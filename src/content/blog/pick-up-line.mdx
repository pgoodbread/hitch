A pick up line has one job: get a reply. Not a laugh, not a nod from
your group chat — a reply. Most lists ignore that and hand you three
hundred puns about being a parking ticket, which is why most people
who use them end up in a thread with no response.

The lines below are sorted by what they're for, and every section
explains what makes it work. Steal them directly if you want. You'll
do better if you use them as templates.

## Why most pick up lines fail

She's not reading your line in isolation. She's reading it in a stack
of forty others, most of which are "hey", "hey beautiful", or the
same parking ticket joke you were about to send.

That's the real competition. A line doesn't have to be brilliant, it
has to be obviously written for her. The moment a message could have
been sent to anyone, it gets treated like it was — which, statistically,
it was.

There's a second failure mode that has nothing to do with the line.
If you're getting very few matches in the first place, no opener is
reaching anyone. A pick up line is a conversation problem, not a
match problem. If people are
[swiping left](/blog/swipe-left-meaning) before they read anything,
the fix is upstream in your
[photos](/blog/profile-pictures) and your
[bio](/blog/best-tinder-bios-for-guys).

## What makes a pick up line work

Three things, in order of how much they matter:

- **It's specific to her.** One detail from her profile is worth more
  than any amount of cleverness. It proves you looked.
- **It's easy to answer.** Give her something to react to — a claim to
  correct, a choice to make, a mild accusation to defend. "Hey" makes
  her do all the work.
- **It has a tone.** Playful, dry, curious. Anything with a personality
  beats anything neutral, even when the neutral version is more
  polished.

Notice what's missing: being smooth. Smoothness reads as practice, and
practice reads as volume. A slightly awkward line about her cat
outperforms a perfect line about her eyes almost every time.

## Funny pick up lines

Funny works because it's low-pressure. She can reply with a joke and
hasn't committed to anything yet, which makes replying cheap.

> I have to be honest, I swiped right because of the photo with the
> enormous fish. I have questions, and one of them is about the fish.

Works because it names a specific photo and promises a conversation
rather than starting one with a compliment.

> Your profile is three concerts and a dog. I feel like I already know
> how this weekend went for you.

Works because it's an observation she can confirm or deny in one line,
which is the lowest-effort reply there is.

> Bold of you to put "great at trivia" in your bio with no evidence
> attached. I'm going to need a sample question.

Works because it's a light accusation. Things you can defend yourself
against are much easier to answer than compliments.

> I'm not saying your bio is a red flag, I'm saying I read it twice
> and I'm still thinking about it. What does "aggressively normal"
> mean.

Works because it admits she got your attention and hands her a single
question to answer.

## Flirty pick up lines that aren't creepy

Flirty is fine. The line between flirty and creepy is whether the
compliment is about her body or about her — a judgment call people
somehow get wrong constantly.

> The hiking photo has you smiling politely and the karaoke photo has
> you genuinely delighted. I'm choosing to believe karaoke is the real
> you.

Works because it compares two photos, which is proof of attention, and
makes a claim she can happily correct.

> Anyone who lists "will judge your coffee order" has to follow
> through. Oat flat white. Go.

Works because it takes her own bio line seriously and gives her an
instant opening.

> You seem like trouble in a very specific and well-organised way and
> I'd like to know more about the system.

Works because it's a compliment about her character, which almost
nobody in her inbox is bothering with.

## Cheesy pick up lines, and when they land

Cheesy lines get mocked because people send them straight. Sent with a
wink — that is, sent with an admission that you know exactly how bad
it is — they can work fine.

> I'm going to say something terrible and then we can both move past
> it: are you a parking ticket? Because you've got FINE written all
> over you. Okay. Hi. How's your week going.

Works because the self-aware framing turns a bad line into a bit, and
the real question at the end gives her something to answer.

> Rating my own opener before you do: 4/10, workshopped it for
> minutes, still went with it.

Works because it's a joke about trying, and trying is attractive when
you're honest about it.

The rule for cheesy: the joke is that you sent it, not the line
itself. If you're not framing it, don't send it.

## The highest-hit-rate opener isn't a pick up line at all

The single best-performing opener on a dating app is a specific
reaction to one thing in her profile, with no line attached.

> Three dogs and a "no small talk" rule, so we're going straight to
> full personality breakdowns for all three.

> You put Lisbon and Osaka in the same photo set. I need to know which
> one you'd go back to first.

There's no setup and no punchline. It reads as a person who read her
profile and had a thought — which is rare enough in her inbox to stand
out on its own. Our [rizz lines guide](/blog/best-rizz-lines) has a
larger set of these grouped by what you're reacting to.

The trade-off is that it takes thirty seconds per match instead of
zero. That's the whole cost, and it's the reason it works.

## Lines that get you left on read

- **Anything sexual in the opener.** It's the most common message she
  gets from men she hasn't spoken to and the easiest to ignore.
- **Copy-pasted spam.** If you send the same line to everyone, it will
  eventually reach someone who's seen it. It also sounds like it, even
  to people who haven't.
- **Negging.** The backhanded-compliment tactic is widely recognised
  now and reads as a warning sign rather than a challenge. It's worth
  understanding [why negging backfires](/blog/negging) if you've been
  told it works.
- **Pure compliments about her appearance.** Not offensive, just
  interchangeable. There's nothing in "you're gorgeous" for her to
  reply to.
- **"Hey" on its own.** It's not an opener, it's a request for her to
  write one.

## What to do when she replies

A good pick up line buys you one exchange. What you do with it decides
the rest.

Answer the thing she actually said, add something of your own, and
avoid turning it into an interview. Two or three exchanges in, while
the thread still has energy, move it forward — the details are in our
guide to [asking her out on Tinder](/blog/ask-girl-out-tinder). Threads
that run for two weeks without a plan rarely become anything.

If the [rizz](/blog/rizz) question is nagging at you: it's the same
skill described with newer slang. Specific, playful, easy to answer.

## The takeaway

A pick up line works when it's clearly aimed at one person, easy to
answer, and has a tone. Funny, flirty and cheesy all work within those
rules, and all fail outside them. The profile-specific opener beats
every scripted line, at the cost of thirty seconds of reading.

And if the openers aren't the problem — if you're barely matching in
the first place — no line fixes that. An
[AI profile review](/) grades your photos, their order and your bio
together, and tells you which half is actually costing you matches.
