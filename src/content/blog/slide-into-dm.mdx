To slide into a DM is to message someone privately who hasn't invited
you to — usually on Instagram, sometimes on Tinder after a match, and
occasionally on whatever app the two of you both happen to be on. The
phrase is half-joking because the move has a reputation: most people
who slide into DMs do it badly, and the DM sits there unread.

The move itself is fine. It's how people meet. What fails is the
execution, and the execution fails in roughly the same four ways every
time. Here's what to send, what to send it on, and what to do when it
works.

## What it means to slide into a DM

It means starting a private conversation cold. There was no
introduction, no group chat, no "my friend gave me your Instagram" —
you saw their profile, you decided to say something, and you said it.

That's the whole move. The reputation comes from volume: the average
attractive account gets a steady stream of these, and almost all of
them are some version of "hey" or a compliment about their face. Your
message isn't being read on its own. It's being read against the
twenty before it.

Which is good news, actually. The bar is not high. You don't need to
be charming — you need to be obviously not one of the twenty.

## They'll look at your profile before they answer

This is the part people skip. Nobody replies to a DM based on the DM
alone. They read it, they tap your name, and they decide from what
they see there whether this conversation is worth having.

So your message is doing maybe forty percent of the work. The rest is
your profile — your
[photos](/blog/profile-pictures), what you post, whether your account
looks like a person or a ghost. If your profile is three gym mirror
shots and a car, a good opener doesn't rescue it.

If you're sending DMs and getting nothing back at any volume, the
problem is almost never the line. Fix upstream first.

## Instagram: react to something, don't announce yourself

The best Instagram DM isn't an opener at all. It's a reply to a story.

Story replies work because they land in the same inbox as a cold DM
but read completely differently. There's an obvious reason you're
messaging — the thing they just posted — and you've handed them an
easy subject to talk about. No introduction needed.

> Okay the dog in that second slide is running a scam on you

> That trail is genuinely the best one in the area and I will not be
> taking questions

> Wait, is that the place on Fifth? I've been trying to get in there
> for a month

Each of those does the same thing: reacts to a specific detail, has a
tone, and is easy to answer. They can send back one line and the
conversation has started.

If there's no story, react to a post instead, and be specific about
it. "Love your feed" is nothing. "The photo from the Lisbon trip is
doing something weird and good with the light" is a real sentence from
a real person.

What doesn't work: the compliment-only DM. "You're gorgeous" is not a
conversation, it's a statement, and the only available replies are
"thanks" or nothing. Most people pick nothing.

## Tinder: the DM is the match message

On Tinder there's no sliding — you matched, so the door is already
open. The same rules apply, but you have a real advantage: their
profile is right there, built specifically to give you something to
talk about.

Use it. One detail from their bio or prompts beats any line you
prepared in advance, and it proves you read past the first photo. If
you want examples of that shape, the
[pick up lines](/blog/pick-up-line) and
[rizz lines](/blog/best-rizz-lines) posts are both organised by what
you're reacting to rather than by how clever the line sounds.

The failure mode on Tinder is different from Instagram. It's not
creepiness, it's blandness — "hey", "how's your weekend going", "you
seem cool". These aren't offensive. They're just nothing, and nothing
gets left in the pile.

## The four ways this goes wrong

**Opening on looks.** It's the most common cold DM and the easiest to
ignore, because they've read it forty times this month and it tells
them nothing about you.

**Double-texting before a reply.** You send the message, an hour
passes, you send "?" or "guess not lol". That second message is the
one that ends it. The first was fine. Wait.

**Writing a paragraph.** A long, thoughtful, three-sentence-minimum
introduction puts the entire burden of the reply on them. Keep it to
one or two lines and let the conversation earn its own length.

**Sliding in and then going quiet.** You got a reply, panicked, and
answered with "haha nice". Have a second thing to say before you send
the first.

## What to actually send

The template, if you want one:

**One specific thing you noticed + a tone + something easy to answer.**

That's it. It works on any platform and it doesn't require you to be
funny, though funny helps because it makes replying cheap — they can
joke back without having committed to anything.

> Your bio says you're "aggressively normal about pasta" and I need
> you to define aggressively

> Three concerts in that photo dump. Which one was actually good and
> which one are you pretending was good

> You've got strong opinions about breakfast food, I can tell. Prove
> me right

Notice none of these are compliments and none of them are questions
about their day. They give the other person a position to take. That's
the whole trick — make replying the path of least resistance.

## When to send it

Less important than people think, but not nothing. Evenings and
weekends beat weekday mornings, because that's when people are
actually in the app rather than checking it between meetings. A story
reply should go out while the story is still up — replying to
something eighteen hours old reads as archaeology.

Beyond that, don't overthink it. A good message sent at a mediocre
time outperforms a mediocre message sent at the perfect one, and
nobody has ever looked at a timestamp and decided against replying.

## Once they reply

The DM worked. Now don't waste it.

Match their energy and their length. If they sent one line, don't send
five back. Keep the thread moving with
[questions that go somewhere](/blog/question-to-ask-your-crush)
instead of interviewing them, and stay on a subject long enough to
actually get somewhere before you change it.

The other thing: have an exit. A DM thread that runs for two weeks
without going anywhere is a DM thread that dies of natural causes. Once
there's real back-and-forth — they're asking you things, the replies
are getting longer — move it forward.
[Asking them out](/blog/ask-girl-out-tinder) at that point is a much
smaller step than it feels like, and it's smaller than it'll feel in
another week. If you've swapped numbers and it's gone to text, the
[morning text](/blog/good-morning-text-for-him) rules are the same
rules: short, specific, not every single day.

## The short version

Slide into DMs by reacting to something real, on a profile that's
worth tapping into, with a message short enough that answering it is
easy. Don't lead with looks, don't double-text, don't write an essay.

The move isn't the problem. It never was. What gets ignored is the
version of it that could have been sent to anybody — because as far as
the person reading it can tell, it was.
