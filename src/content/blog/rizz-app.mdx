A rizz app takes your dating-app conversation and writes the next
message for you. You screenshot the chat, it reads the screenshot, it
hands back three replies you can paste. Some of them also write bios,
some generate openers from a profile, and most of them advertise with
a clip of a guy getting a phone number in four messages.

The honest version: they do one narrow job reasonably well and three
other jobs badly, and the narrow job is rarely the reason you're not
getting dates. Here's what each type actually does, and how to tell
whether you're the person it helps.

## What a rizz app actually does

Almost everything marketed as a rizz app falls into one of three
buckets. They get sold as the same product and they are not.

### Reply generators

You upload a screenshot of a conversation. The app reads it and
produces a few possible next messages, usually tagged with a tone —
flirty, funny, bold.

This is the core product and the one that works best, because the
task is genuinely narrow. The model has the actual conversation in
front of it, so it can see what she said and respond to it. That's
already better than what most people send, which is a generic line
with no connection to the message above it.

The catch is that it's reactive. It needs an existing conversation
to work on. If you're not getting matches, or matches never reply to
your first message, a reply generator has nothing to read.

### Opener generators

You feed it her profile — photos, bio, prompts — and it writes a
first message.

Quality here drops off fast. The good output is a specific reaction
to a specific detail, which is exactly what a good opener is. The bad
output is a clever-sounding line stapled onto whatever noun it found
in her bio, which reads to her like every other message she got that
day.

The failure is easy to spot once you know it. If the line would still
make sense pointed at someone else's profile, it isn't an opener,
it's a pickup line with a costume on. Worked examples of the
difference are in [the rizz lines piece](/blog/best-rizz-lines) and
[the pick up lines one](/blog/pick-up-line) — both are organised
around reacting to something real rather than performing.

### Bio and profile writers

You answer a few questions, it writes your bio.

This is the weakest category, and the reason is structural rather
than technical. A bio works when it contains specific things that are
true about you and that someone can react to. An app that knows six
facts about you will produce six facts arranged prettily, and the
result reads like a product description — polished, generic,
unanswerable.

Bios fail on the same thing conversations do: asserting traits
instead of demonstrating them. "Sarcastic, adventurous, love to
travel" is three assertions and zero hooks. The
[bio guide](/blog/best-tinder-bios-for-guys) walks through what a
demonstration looks like instead, and it's mostly a matter of being
willing to say something narrow.

## Where a rizz app genuinely helps

Three situations, and they're real ones.

**You freeze mid-conversation.** She replied with something flat —
"haha true" — and you have no idea what to do with it. A reply
generator gives you three options in five seconds, which beats
staring at the thread for a day and then sending "so what are you up
to this weekend." Momentum is worth more than wit here.

**You're bad at tone in writing.** Some people are genuinely funnier
in person than in text, and a suggestion that shows what "flirty but
not creepy" looks like in one sentence is a useful calibration. Use
it as a reference, not a script.

**You want volume without going robotic.** If you're running a lot of
conversations, the app helps you stay responsive instead of letting
half of them die from slow replies.

The thread through all three: the app is helping you get unstuck in a
conversation that already exists. That is the job it's actually good
at.

## Where rizz apps reliably fail

**A generated line on a weak profile still gets nothing.** This is
the big one. Messaging only matters after a match, and a match only
happens after your photos and bio do their job. If you get four
matches a month, the ceiling on any messaging tool is four
conversations. Nothing the app writes changes the number above it.

**The voice doesn't survive contact.** A line funnier than you are
sets an expectation you then have to meet for the rest of the
conversation. Message three is where that collapses, and the collapse
is more expensive than a boring opener would have been.

**It has no idea what you actually want.** The app optimises for a
reply, because that's the only signal it can see. A reply isn't the
goal. A plan is. Conversations that get pleasant and never move are
the most common way matches quietly expire, and
[asking her out](/blog/ask-girl-out-tinder) is a decision no
generator will make for you.

**Paste-shaped answers read as paste-shaped.** People who use dating
apps a lot develop a decent ear for generated text — the slightly
over-smooth rhythm, the joke that lands one beat too neatly. Using
suggestions as raw material and rewriting them in your own words
avoids most of this. Pasting verbatim does not.

## The part no rizz app can fix

Your photos are evaluated on every profile you appear on. Your best
message is only read by people who already swiped right.

That asymmetry decides where effort is worth spending, and it's why
the messaging tools are the most-marketed and least-useful layer of
the stack. A clear first photo, a real smile, decent light, and a
lineup that shows a person with a life — that's what changes your
match count, and it's covered properly in
[the photo guide](/blog/profile-pictures).

None of this is an argument that messaging doesn't matter. It's an
argument about order. Fix the thing that runs on every impression
before you optimise the thing that runs on a few dozen.

## How to tell whether you need one

Two numbers, and you probably already know both.

**If you get matches and the conversations die**, your messaging is
the weak link. A reply generator is a reasonable thing to try, and
the four habits in the [rizz explainer](/blog/rizz) will get you
further for free — react to one specific thing, keep it short, leave
her something answerable, sound like yourself.

**If you barely get matches**, no rizz app is reaching anyone. The
problem is upstream, in the photos and the bio, and a messaging tool
is a subscription that fixes a bottleneck you don't have.

Most people guess wrong about which situation they're in, usually
because messaging is the part you can see failing and photos fail
silently. If you'd rather not guess, an [AI profile review](/) grades
the photos, their order and the bio together and tells you which half
is costing you the matches.

## The takeaway

A rizz app is a reply generator with a marketing budget. The reply
generation is genuinely useful when a conversation stalls, the opener
generation is hit and miss, and the bio writing is the weakest thing
in the box.

None of it is a substitute for a profile that earns the match in the
first place. Use one to get unstuck, rewrite what it gives you so it
sounds like you, and spend the bigger share of your effort one layer
up.
