You sent something you thought was decent. She sent back "lol". Then
"yeah". Then nothing for six hours. The dry texting meaning question
almost always arrives at this exact moment — three flat replies in,
trying to work out whether you've been quietly rejected or whether
she's just at work.

Here's what dry texting is, the five things it usually means, how to
tell them apart, and what to send next. Including the version nobody
wants to hear: a fair amount of the time, the dry texter is you.

## Dry texting meaning, in one line

Dry texting is texting that gives the other person nothing to reply
to. Short answers, no questions back, no new information. The thread
technically continues, but one person is carrying all of it.

That's the whole definition. What it _means_ is a separate question,
and it has about five answers — only one of which is "she's not
interested."

## What it actually looks like

Dry texting is easier to recognise than to define:

> you: how was the hike?
>
> her: good
>
> you: nice — did you do the whole loop?
>
> her: yeah

Nothing rude happened there. She answered both questions. But she
added no detail, asked nothing back, and left you to invent message
three out of thin air. Do that four times and the conversation dies of
exhaustion.

The usual tells:

- **One-word replies.** "k", "lol", "nice", "haha yeah".
- **No questions back.** You're asking everything.
- **No new information.** She answers exactly what was asked and not a
  word more.
- **Gaps that keep growing.** Twenty minutes, then two hours, then the
  next morning.
- **Agreement with everything.** "yeah totally" to four messages in a
  row is not enthusiasm, it's politeness on autopilot.

## The five things dry texting usually means

### She has no bandwidth right now

The least dramatic explanation and a common one. She's working, with
friends, or texting three other people. Short replies cost nothing;
good ones cost attention she hasn't got at 2pm on a Tuesday.

This version comes back. If the thread picks up in the evening, that's
all it ever was.

### She's mirroring your energy

People match the effort they're given, roughly and unconsciously. If
your last four messages were "hey", "wyd", "haha nice" and "cool", she
is not going to write you a paragraph about her week.

This one feels like rejection and isn't. It's an echo.

### The conversation has nothing in it

Some threads are dry from message one because there's no subject.
"Hey, how's your weekend going" produces "good you?" and then both of
you are stuck. Nobody did anything wrong — there's just no material.

This is the most fixable version, and the fix is subject matter rather
than technique.

### She's letting it die politely

Most people don't send "I'm not interested." They reduce effort until
you stop, because that's the version that avoids a conversation.
Replies get shorter, gaps get longer, questions stop entirely.

The honest signal here isn't any single message, it's the direction of
travel over the last ten.

### That's just how she texts

Plenty of people are flat on text and perfectly warm in person. If her
replies were short on day one, day three and day five, with no change
in slope, you're reading a texting style, not a verdict. The giveaway
is that she still replies quickly and still says yes to plans.

## How to tell which one you've got

Two questions settle it most of the time.

**Is it shrinking?** Compare her messages now with her messages at the
start. Paragraph → sentence → word → nothing is a thread ending. Short
replies that have been short the whole time are a baseline, not a
decline.

**Who's asking the questions?** If you've asked the last five and
she's asked none, effort is one-directional and you already know what
that means. If she's still asking things — even small things — she's
in it.

What isn't a signal: reply speed on its own. People put their phones
face down. The same trap catches guys reading
[the ellipsis](/blog/meaning-in-text-from-a-girl) for hidden meaning —
one message in isolation carries far less information than it feels
like it does.

## You might be the dry texter

Worth sitting with for a second. Most guys asking about dry texting
are also doing it, usually without noticing.

The tells:

- You reply "haha nice" to things that deserved an actual response.
- You ask questions her profile already answered.
- Every message of yours is a question, which turns the thread into an
  interview.
- You keep replies deliberately short because somebody told you
  mystery is attractive.
- You wait an hour to answer, on purpose, as a tactic.

The fix is unglamorous: answer with a detail, then hand something
back. Not more words — more surface area.

> her: good
>
> you: that's the one with the lake at the top? I did it in completely
> the wrong shoes last summer and regretted every step

Now there are three things to reply to instead of zero. That's the
entire skill. [What to talk about](/blog/what-to-talk-about-with-a-girl)
matters more than how you phrase it.

## How to restart a dry thread

One message. New subject. Easy to answer. Ideally something only she
can answer.

> you: random, but your photos have you in Lisbon — worth going or
> overrated? deciding between there and Porto

That works because it's specific, it's light, and answering it takes
four seconds. Humour does the same job if it's natural to you —
[a funny text](/blog/funny-texts) lands when it's short and cheap to
reply to, not when it's a setup with a punchline she has to applaud.

Three things that don't work: the double text asking if she's still
there, the "wow, okay" guilt message, and the paragraph explaining how
you've noticed her energy has changed. All three make the thread about
the thread, which is the one subject nobody wants.

If the restart gets a real reply — longer, with a question in it —
you're back. That's the moment to move
[the conversation forward](/blog/tinder-messages) rather than
celebrate, because threads have a shelf life and a revived one is on
borrowed time. Ask her out while it's warm.

And when it's genuinely going well, you can stop auditing every
message and just be
[a bit flirty](/blog/flirty-texts-for-her). Dry threads are rarely
killed by the wrong joke. They're killed by two people being careful
at each other.

## When to let it go

If the restart lands flat — another one-word reply, no question back —
that's your answer. Don't send a second one. Nobody has ever been
talked back into interest by persistence, and the fourth unanswered
message is the one that gets screenshotted.

Letting it sit is also not a tactic. She might resurface in a week.
She might not. Either way the correct move is the same, which is to
stop refreshing the thread and go talk to someone else.

## If every thread goes dry, it isn't the threads

The pattern matters more than the conversation. If this keeps
happening with different people, the problem is upstream of the
texting.

Usually it's the [opener](/blog/tinder-openers). A conversation that
starts with "hey" or a compliment has no subject in it, so it runs out
of road by message four and dies of boredom — and that reads as dry
texting when it's actually a thread that was never alive.

The other cause is the profile. People decide how much effort a reply
is worth by tapping your name first. Thin profile, low-priority
replies. Fix those two and the decoding problem mostly stops
happening, because conversations that started with something real
don't go dry in the first place.

## The short version

Dry texting means replies with nothing to answer — and it usually
means she's busy, she's mirroring you, the topic is empty, she's
easing out, or that's just how she types. Read the direction of
travel, not one message. Add detail instead of asking another
question. Try one good restart, and if it lands flat, leave it there.
