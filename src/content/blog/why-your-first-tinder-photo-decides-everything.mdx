Nobody reads your bio first. They see one photo, at thumbnail size, for
about a second — and most of the decision is already made.

That's not a reason to panic. It's a reason to stop treating your photo
order like an afterthought. I've reviewed hundreds of profiles, and the
single most common mistake isn't bad photos. It's good photos in the
wrong order.

## What actually happens in that first second

When someone hits your profile, three questions get answered almost
instantly:

1. **Can I see your face clearly?** Sunglasses, group shots, and
   far-away hiking photos all fail this test.
2. **Do you look like someone I'd feel comfortable meeting?** Genuine
   expression beats posed smolder every time.
3. **Did you put in effort?** Blurry mirror selfies signal the
   opposite, no matter how good you look in them.

If your first photo fails any of these, the swipe happens before photos
two through six ever load.

## The photos that keep losing

Some first-photo choices are so common they're worth calling out
directly:

- **The group shot.** Nobody wants to play "guess which one." Even if
  you're the best-looking person in the photo, you've added friction.
- **The car selfie.** It reads as "I couldn't be bothered to take one
  real photo."
- **The gym mirror.** There's a place for a physique photo. Slot one
  later in the lineup — leading with it filters out more people than it
  attracts.
- **The sunglasses photo.** Eyes carry most of the trust signal in a
  face. Hide them and you're a stranger, not a match.

## What to lead with instead

Your first photo should be a clear, well-lit shot of your face and
shoulders, with a genuine expression, taken by someone else (or at
least looking like it was). That's it. Not your most impressive photo —
your most *legible* one.

Impressive photos — the skydive, the stage, the summit — work brilliantly
in slots two through four, where they add depth to someone who already
decided your face is worth a second look.

## A quick test you can run tonight

Pull up your profile and look at your first photo at thumbnail size —
actually shrink it down. Then ask:

- Would a stranger know what you look like from this photo alone?
- Is your expression one you'd wear meeting a friend, or one you
  rehearsed?
- Is there anything in the frame that needs explaining?

If you hesitated on any of those, swap in a simpler photo and give it a
week. Most guys see a noticeable difference in match rate from this one
change — before touching the bio at all.

The first photo isn't about looking your best. It's about being easy to
say yes to.
