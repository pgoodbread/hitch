Nearly every guide to dirty texts is a list of lines. Ninety-seven of
them, sorted into categories, written for nobody in particular. The
lines are not the problem. The problem is that most guys send a
perfectly good one about four days before she was ready for it, and
then spend the next week wondering why a thread that was going well
went quiet.

Escalation is a timing skill wearing a vocabulary costume. Get the
timing right and a fairly plain sentence does enormous work. Get it
wrong and the cleverest line you have ever written reads as a man who
was only ever waiting for an opening.

## When dirty texts actually work

There is one condition, and everything else is detail: she has already
escalated, or she has clearly invited you to.

Not "she replied." Not "she seemed friendly." She moved the register
herself — a double entendre she did not have to make, a compliment
about how you look, a joke that had an edge on it, a question about
your last relationship that was not really about your last
relationship. That is the signal. It is unmistakable when it happens,
which is why the guys who miss it are usually guys who sent something
before it arrived.

The second condition is that the thread is warm. Momentum matters more
here than anywhere else in texting. An escalation into a conversation
you have been having for forty minutes reads as the next step. The
identical text into a thread that last moved on Sunday reads as a man
scrolling back through his messages looking for someone to text at
eleven at night.

If neither condition is met, the move is not a better line. It is
[flirty texts](/blog/flirty-texts-for-her) — the tier below, where you
tease and compliment and give her room to come up a level herself. She
will, if she is interested. Letting her set the pace costs you one day
and saves you the entire thread.

## Read her replies, not your nerve

The signals are in what comes back, and they are legible if you look.

**She matches or raises.** You sent something suggestive, she sent
something more suggestive. That is a green light and the only
unambiguous one. Stay at her level or just under it. Do not take a
green light as permission to jump three rungs.

**She deflects with humour.** A joke that moves the subject sideways
is not rejection, it is a request to slow down. The correct response
is to follow the joke, not to repeat the escalation in different
words. You can come back to it tomorrow. She will remember.

**She answers the words and ignores the temperature.** You sent a
loaded line, she replied about her weekend. That is a polite no for
now. One of those is fine. Two in a row means you are the only person
in the conversation having it.

**The replies get short.** One word, a reaction emoji, a thumbs up.
You escalated too fast and she is withdrawing without making it a
scene. That is the single clearest signal in texting, and most guys
read it as her being busy. It usually is not —
[dry texting](/blog/dry-texting-meaning) has a diagnosis, and this is
one of the common causes.

The hardest ones to read are the ambiguous replies, especially the
ones that trail off mid-sentence. An ellipsis is doing something
specific, and [what it means](/blog/meaning-in-text-from-a-girl) is
almost never the thing guys hope it means.

## Four patterns, with the explicit part left out

What follows are shapes, not scripts. Every one of them works by
implying rather than describing, which is also why they are safer: an
implication can be laughed off by either of you, so neither of you has
to be embarrassed if the timing was wrong.

### The delayed thought

Tell her something crossed your mind hours after it happened.

> Genuinely had to stop what I was doing earlier because I thought
> about that thing you said last night. Unhelpful of you.

> You've been distracting me since about two o'clock and you weren't
> even here. Take some responsibility.

The variable is the moment you are referring back to. It works because
it is flattering without being a compliment, and because it tells her
she has been on your mind without the word "thinking" doing any heavy
lifting.

### The specific memory

If something has already happened between you, be precise about one
small part of it.

> Still thinking about the thirty seconds before you got in the taxi.
> That's all I'm going to say about that.

> That jacket is going to be a problem for me and I want that on the
> record.

The variable is the detail. The restraint is the point — naming one
narrow thing and then stopping is considerably more effective than
narrating the whole evening back at her.

### The withheld detail

Start something and refuse to finish it.

> I had a very specific thought about Friday and I've decided you have
> to ask for it.

> There's a version of this conversation I'd rather have in person.

The variable is the thing you are not saying. This pattern hands her
the next move, which is useful for exactly the reason it sounds like a
dodge: if she asks, you have consent and momentum; if she changes the
subject, nothing was ever said out loud.

### The direct one, once it is earned

When she has escalated twice and you are both clearly in the same
conversation, stop being clever.

> I'd like to see you tonight and I'm not going to pretend it's about
> the wine.

Directness is a higher tier than any innuendo, and it only works on
top of everything above it. Sent cold it is a proposition from a
stranger. Sent into a thread where she has been escalating for an
hour, it is the most attractive thing in this article, because it is
the only one that is not hiding.

## When a dirty text lands badly

It will happen. The recovery matters more than the mistake.

Do not apologise at length. A paragraph of contrition turns a
misjudged line into a scene and makes her manage your feelings about
it. Do not double-text to explain what you meant. And do not pretend
it did not happen by sending a cheerful good-morning text the next day
as though the thread were not sitting there.

What works is one short line that gives her an exit, and then normal
conversation.

> Read the room badly there. Anyway — did you survive the thing with
> your sister?

That is it. You acknowledged it in six words, you handed her an easy
subject, and you demonstrated the thing that was missing from the text
she did not like: that you can tolerate a no. Plenty of threads
survive this. Almost none survive the three-message apology.

The one thing that does not come back is the late-night cold open —
the 1am message into a dead thread with nothing before it. That is not
a dirty text, it is a transaction request without a cover story, and
it is read as exactly that. If you want to understand the pacing
underneath all of this,
[what to send and when](/blog/tinder-messages) is the longer version.

## This is a profile problem more often than a texting problem

Here is the part the line lists leave out.

Escalation feels sudden when nothing before it set it up. If your
photos say "corporate headshot and a group shot at a wedding" and your
bio says "love to travel, ask me anything," then the first suggestive
text you send has arrived from nowhere, because nothing in your
profile suggested a man who flirts. She is not reacting to the line.
She is reacting to the gap between the line and everything she knew
about you before it.

The guys who can escalate early are not braver. They have profiles
with an edge in them — a photo with real expression, a bio with a joke
that has some teeth. By the time they send something suggestive, it
reads as consistent rather than as a switch being flipped.

The same applies to volume. If you are getting two matches a month,
every thread carries the weight of a last chance, and that pressure is
audible in the texts. Fix the supply and your texting improves without
you learning a single new line. The reverse — better lines, same
profile — almost never works, which is also why
[a funny text](/blog/funny-texts) is the safer opening escalation for
most men: it tests the temperature at no cost.

And if you are on the other side of this, wondering whether to answer
one, the logic runs the same direction. Escalation in the
[morning](/blog/good-morning-text-for-him) or at midnight is the same
question: did anything before it earn this.

## The short version

Dirty texts work when she escalated first, the thread is warm, and you
said less than you wanted to. Imply rather than describe, match her
level instead of outrunning it, and read the reply before you decide
what comes next.

If one lands badly, acknowledge it in one line and move on. And if
every thread you escalate goes quiet, the problem is upstream of the
text — she needs to have already believed you were that guy before you
sent it.
