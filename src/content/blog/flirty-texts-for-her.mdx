Most flirty texts for her fail for the same boring reason: she has read
them before. The lines that circulate on the internet circulate to
everybody, which means the compliment you spent ten minutes picking is
sitting in her thread three times already, sent by three other guys who
also thought it was clever.

What actually works is less impressive and harder to copy. A flirty
text lands when it could only have been sent by you, to her,
today. That's the whole trick. Everything below is a way of getting
there without sounding like you workshopped it.

## What separates flirty from try-hard

Flirty is confident indifference to the outcome. Try-hard is the same
sentence sent by someone who needs it to work.

She can tell the difference instantly, and not from the words. She
tells from the volume. One text that teases her and then stops is
flirty. The same text followed by "haha" and then "you there?" forty
minutes later is a man asking for reassurance, which is the least
attractive thing you can send at any hour.

So the first rule is structural, not verbal: say the thing, then let it
sit. The silence after a flirty text is doing half the work. If you
cannot tolerate forty minutes of it, the text is not ready to send.

The second rule is specificity. "You're gorgeous" is a fact about your
opinion. "That photo of you mid-laugh at whatever is happening off
camera is doing far too much work in this profile" is a fact about
her. The first one she has heard a hundred times. The second one she
has heard once, from you.

## Timing beats wording

A mediocre text at the right moment outperforms a great one at the
wrong moment, and it isn't close.

The right moments are narrow:

- **Within a few hours of a good exchange.** Momentum is real. A
  flirty text into a warm thread reads as continuation; the same text
  into a thread that died on Tuesday reads as a cold open.
- **Early evening, roughly 6pm to 10pm.** She's off work, she's on her
  phone, she has attention to spare. Mid-morning competes with her job
  and loses.
- **After she gave you something.** A joke, a story, a photo of her
  dog. Flirting works best as a response to an offer, not as an
  opening bid.

The wrong moments are mostly one moment: late at night, with nothing
preceding it. A 1am "hey you up" is not a flirty text. It is a
transaction request with no cover story, and she will read it as
exactly that.

If you want the morning slot to work, it needs its own logic — the
[good morning text](/blog/good-morning-text-for-him) pattern is the
same idea pointed the other way, and the reason it works is that it
says something rather than just arriving.

## Five flirty text patterns, with the variable called out

Learn the shapes rather than the lines. Each one below has a blank you
fill from her actual profile or your actual conversation. Fill it with
something generic and the pattern stops working — that's a feature, it
keeps you honest.

### The callback tease

Reference something she said and refuse to let it go.

> Still not over the fact that you ranked airports. I need the bottom
> three and I need you to defend them.

> I've thought about your pizza take more today than I thought about
> work. This is your fault and I'd like compensation.

The variable is the thing she said. The flirt is in the mock
grievance — you're treating a throwaway comment as a serious matter
between you, which creates a shared joke out of nothing.

### The specific compliment

One detail, observed, not rated.

> You have the exact face of someone who has strong opinions about the
> thermostat and I respect it.

> Whoever took that second photo deserves a raise. You look like
> you're getting away with something.

The variable is the detail. Notice neither of these says beautiful,
gorgeous or stunning. Compliment a choice she made — an expression, a
jacket, a caption — and it lands as attention. Compliment her face in
the abstract and it lands as a transaction.

### The playful accusation

Accuse her of something small and obviously untrue.

> You're one of those people who claps when the plane lands, aren't
> you. Be honest.

> Something tells me you've never once returned a shopping trolley.
> I'm prepared to be proven wrong.

The variable is the crime. This one is the highest-reply-rate pattern
on the list because defending yourself is irresistible. Keep the
accusation trivial. Accuse her of anything that could actually be true
and insulting, and you've started a different conversation.

### The plan-adjacent tease

Flirt with the idea of meeting without asking yet.

> If you tell me you also like that terrible bar on the corner I'm
> going to have to take this seriously.

> Noted that you claim to make the best carbonara in the city. Bold
> claim. Unverified.

The variable is the shared thing. This is the pattern to use when
you're a few days in and the thread needs direction — it points at a
date without putting one on the table, so there's nothing to turn
down. When she plays along, that's your opening to
[ask her out](/blog/ask-girl-out-tinder) properly.

### The low-stakes challenge

Give her something to win.

> Okay, settle it. Best film of the last ten years, one answer, no
> hedging. If you say The Godfather I'm blocking you.

> Pick one: never coffee again or never your phone in bed again. You
> have to choose.

The variable is the question. It works because it's easy to answer and
impossible to answer boringly. It also hands the thread somewhere to
go, which solves the real problem most people have —
[what to talk about](/blog/what-to-talk-about-with-a-girl) once the
openers are done.

## Where flirty texts for her go wrong

Every dead thread I've seen died from one of these.

**Double-texting into silence.** You sent something good, she hasn't
replied in two hours, so you send a follow-up. The follow-up erases
the first text. It tells her you've been watching the thread, which
turns a confident message into an anxious one retroactively. Wait. If
she doesn't answer, she saw it.

**Escalating faster than she is.** Match her register. If she's
sending two sentences and a joke, send two sentences and a joke. Going
explicit while she's still being playful doesn't speed anything up, it
just makes her decide earlier than she wanted to.

**Compliment stacking.** Three compliments in a row stops being
flattering and starts being a job interview you're losing. One per
thread, maximum, and make it about a choice she made.

**Copy-paste lines.** Canned material has a specific smell: it's always
slightly too polished for the conversation it's in. There is a place
for having [good lines](/blog/best-rizz-lines) in your head as
examples of structure, but sending one verbatim means she's
reading something written for a stranger. She'll notice the seam.

## Read the reply before you send the next one

The reply tells you how much room you have, and most guys don't read
it.

Long answer with a question at the end: she's in, keep going at the
same pace. Short answer with no question: she's being polite, change
something — ask about her, not about the joke. One-word answer or a
reaction emoji: the thread is cooling, give it a day and come back
with something that doesn't require effort to answer.

The ambiguous ones are the trouble, and the ellipsis is the worst
offender. If she's trailing off mid-sentence, that
[means something specific](/blog/meaning-in-text-from-a-girl) and it's
usually not what guys assume.

One more thing worth saying plainly. If you're sending flirty texts
into threads that keep dying, and you're getting few threads to begin
with, the texting isn't the bottleneck. Conversations are easier when
she already liked what she saw — the
[opener](/blog/tinder-openers) and the profile behind it do most of the
work before you type a word. Same goes for the cold cases, where
there's no match at all and you're
[sliding into DMs](/blog/slide-into-dm) on hope alone.

## The short version

Flirty texts for her work when they're specific, timed into a warm
thread, and sent once. Tease something she actually said, accuse her of
something trivial, compliment a choice rather than her face, and then
put the phone down.

The line matters less than the restraint after it. Say the thing, let
it sit, and read what comes back before you decide what to send next.
