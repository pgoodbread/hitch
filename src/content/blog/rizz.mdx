Rizz means charm. Specifically, it means the kind of charm that gets
someone interested in you romantically — the ability to talk to a
person you're attracted to and have it go well.

That's the whole definition. It's short for "charisma," it lost the
first syllable somewhere on the internet, and it now works as a noun
you have, a thing you can win or lose, and occasionally a verb.

The definition takes ten seconds. The more useful question — the one
that sent most people to this page — is what rizz actually looks like
in practice, and whether it's something you can build rather than
something you were born with. That's the back half of this article.

## Rizz meaning, in one line

Rizz is romantic charm: the skill of making a conversation with
someone you're into feel easy and interesting for them.

Two things worth pinning down, because they get muddled constantly:

- **It's about them, not you.** A guy with rizz isn't the funniest
  person in the room. He's the one the other person enjoys talking
  to. Those are different skills and only one of them gets you a
  second date.
- **It's not a script.** People search for rizz expecting a set of
  lines. Lines are the visible part. The thing underneath is paying
  attention and responding to what's actually in front of you.

## Where the word came from

It's a clipping of "charisma" — you drop the front and the end and
keep the middle. Streamer Kai Cenat popularised it around 2021 and it
spread through Twitch and TikTok fast enough that Oxford University
Press named it their word of the year in 2023.

The etymology matters slightly more than usual here, because it
explains the shape of the word. Charisma isn't a line you deliver.
It's how you come across across an entire interaction. Rizz inherited
that, which is why "he has rizz" makes sense and "he said a rizz"
doesn't.

## W rizz, L rizz, and unspoken rizz

Three variants do most of the work in actual usage:

**W rizz** — "W" for win. It went well. She replied, she laughed, the
conversation kept going. Used as a compliment or a self-report after a
good exchange.

**L rizz** — "L" for loss. It went badly. Left on read, one-word
answer, unmatched. Usually said about someone else, occasionally
about yourself, always slightly cruelly.

**Unspoken rizz** — attraction that happens without you saying
anything clever, or anything at all. Presence, looks, how you carry
yourself, the vibe of a photo. It's a joke term about half the time,
but it points at something real: a lot of what decides whether
someone's interested happens before the first message.

You'll also see **rizz god** (someone unusually good at this),
**negative rizz** (actively making it worse), and **rizzing someone
up** (the verb, meaning to flirt with them successfully).

## What actually reads as rizz on a dating profile

Here's where the definition stops being trivia. On an app, nobody
gets to experience your charm in real time. They get four to six
photos, a few lines of text, and about three seconds. All of your
rizz has to survive that compression.

Which means it splits into two halves that fail for completely
different reasons.

## Unspoken rizz is mostly your photos

The joke term is the accurate one. Before you type a single word,
your photos have already made most of the case — how relaxed you
look, whether you're actually in your own pictures, whether the
lineup shows a person with a life or a person with a camera.

The specifics of what works are their own article, and I've written
[that one](/blog/profile-pictures). The short version: the
[first photo does most of the deciding](/blog/why-your-first-tinder-photo-decides-everything),
and "confident and legible" beats "impressive" every time. A clear
face, a real smile, decent light. That's unspoken rizz, and it isn't
a mystery — it's a checklist.

The reason this half matters more than the messaging half is
volume. Photos are evaluated on every single profile you appear on.
Your best line is only read by people who already swiped right.

## The rizz you can write down

The written half lives in your bio and your prompts, and the
failure mode is almost universal: guys assert traits instead of
demonstrating them.

"Sarcastic" is an assertion. A sarcastic sentence is a
demonstration. "Love to travel" is an assertion. "Will absolutely
lose an argument about which country does breakfast best" is a
demonstration and also an invitation to argue.

That's the entire trick, and it's why prompt-style answers work so
well —
[a "dating me is like" answer](/blog/dating-me-is-like) forces you
into a specific image instead of an adjective. If you want the
longer treatment of what to actually put in the text,
[the bio guide](/blog/best-tinder-bios-for-guys) covers it.

The other thing written rizz does is leave hooks. Every specific
detail you include is something someone can react to. An empty bio
gives a match nothing to open with, which quietly caps how many
conversations you ever start — the same problem from the other side
of the screen.

## Rizz in the conversation itself

Once you're matched, the pattern that works is boring and
consistent:

1. **React to something specific.** One detail from her profile,
   responded to directly. This matters more than everything else
   combined.
2. **Keep it short.** One or two sentences. Long openers read as
   effort you'll expect repaid.
3. **Make it easy to answer.** A claim she can dispute, an opinion
   she has, a question with a real answer. Compliments are pleasant
   and unanswerable.
4. **Sound like yourself.** If the line is funnier than you are, the
   conversation collapses on message three.

If you want worked examples of all four, the
[rizz lines article](/blog/best-rizz-lines) is a set of them grouped
by what you're reacting to. And when it's going well, rizz has a
job: it's supposed to end in a plan.
[Asking her out](/blog/ask-girl-out-tinder) is the part most people
stall on, usually by waiting for a perfect moment that doesn't
arrive.

## Can you actually build rizz?

Yes, and the framing of the word is what makes people think
otherwise. "Having rizz" sounds like a fixed trait, the way "being
tall" is. In practice almost all of it is habit.

Reading a profile before messaging is a habit. Writing specifically
instead of generically is a habit. Sending one line and then leaving
it alone is a habit. Getting photos taken in daylight by a friend
instead of using a three-year-old mirror selfie is a decision you
make once.

None of that requires being naturally charming. It requires
noticing what you're currently doing on autopilot, which is
harder than it sounds and is why most guys plateau.

## The mistake people make with the definition

Searching "rizz" and landing on a lines listicle is how most people
meet this word, and it sets up the wrong model. Lines are the last
five percent. Someone with genuinely good rizz and no memorised
lines does fine. Someone with thirty memorised lines and photos that
don't get swipes never gets to use any of them.

If you're getting matches and conversations die, your messaging is
the problem and the four rules above are the fix. If you're barely
getting matches, no amount of rizz is reaching anyone — the problem
is upstream, in the photos and the bio.

Most people guess wrong about which one they have. If you'd rather
not guess, an [AI profile review](/) grades the photos, the order
and the bio together and tells you which half is actually costing
you.

## The takeaway

Rizz means romantic charm — short for charisma, popularised by
streamers, Oxford's word of the year in 2023. W rizz went well, L
rizz didn't, unspoken rizz is the part that happens before you say
anything.

On a dating app it splits cleanly: unspoken rizz is your photos,
written rizz is being specific instead of generic. Both are
learnable. Neither is a line you memorise.
