How many likes do you get on Tinder? On a free account, somewhere
around 100 right swipes in a 12-hour window. Tinder has never published
a fixed number, it moves around by account and region, and the app
itself will only ever tell you that you're out — not how many you
started with.

That vagueness is deliberate, and it's worth understanding before you
start counting. The limit isn't really about rationing swipes. It's
about what the app does with the ones you have left.

## How many likes do you get on Tinder for free

The working answer most free users land on is roughly 100 right swipes
per 12 hours. Some people report hitting a wall closer to 50. A few
swipe well past 100 without seeing anything.

Three things move the number:

**Your age bracket and country.** Tinder has always priced and
throttled differently by market. The like cap behaves the same way.

**How you swipe.** Right-swiping everything at speed is the single
fastest way to find the low end of the range. Accounts that swipe
selectively tend to find the ceiling is higher than they expected,
because they never get near it.

**Whatever Tinder is currently testing.** The limit is a dial, not a
law. It gets adjusted, and your account can be in a test group without
you knowing.

So treat 100 as a planning number rather than a fact. If you need to
know your own limit precisely, you'll have to find it by hitting it,
and finding it by hitting it is a bad habit for reasons we'll get to.

## When the limit resets

The counter runs on a rolling 12-hour clock from the moment you run
out, not from midnight. Hit the wall at 9pm and you're back in business
around 9am.

When it happens you get a full-screen message offering you Tinder Gold
and a countdown. The countdown is honest — it's the only part of this
the app tells you straight.

There's no way to reset it early, no trick with reinstalling the app,
and logging out doesn't help. Deleting and recreating your account to
dodge the limit is the worst version of this idea: you lose every
match, every conversation and all the history the algorithm has built
up on you, in exchange for a few dozen swipes.

## Unused likes don't carry over

The allowance isn't a balance you build up. Swipe twice today and you
don't start tomorrow with 198 — you start with the same cap everyone
else does. There's nothing to bank, which removes the main reason
people give themselves for hoarding.

What does carry over is the likes you've already sent. A right swipe
sits there indefinitely, waiting for the other person to reach your
card in their stack. They may not see it for days, or at all, if they
stop swiping before they get that far. This is why matches sometimes
arrive from people you don't remember liking — the like was sent two
weeks ago and only just landed.

So running out isn't a loss of anything. Your sent likes are still
working while you wait for the clock.

## Super Likes are counted separately

Super Likes run on their own budget and their own clock. Free accounts
get one per day. Paid tiers get a handful, and you can buy more as
credits on any tier.

They don't come out of your regular like allowance, so hitting the
right-swipe wall doesn't cost you your Super Like. It's also the one
like per day that's genuinely scarce, which is a decent argument for
actually spending it rather than saving it for a profile that never
arrives.

## What paying changes

Every paid tier — Plus, Gold, Platinum — includes unlimited likes. That
is the core of what you're buying; the rest of each tier is built
around it.

Worth being honest about what unlimited gets you. It removes a ceiling
you only hit if you swipe a lot, and swiping a lot is not correlated
with matching a lot. If you're running out of likes daily and still not
matching, unlimited likes buys you a faster way to do the thing that
isn't working.

The [honest answer on whether Gold is worth it](/blog/is-tinder-gold-worth-it)
goes through the rest of the package. The short version for this
article: unlimited likes is a convenience feature, not a match
multiplier.

## Why the limit exists at all

Not to sell subscriptions, or at least not only. The limit is a quality
signal.

Tinder's ranking system cares about how selectively you swipe.
[The way the algorithm reads your behaviour](/blog/tinder-algorithm) is
roughly: an account that right-swipes everything is telling the system
its likes mean nothing, so the system stops showing that account to
good profiles. An account that swipes carefully is giving the algorithm
usable information, and gets treated better for it.

The cap enforces selectivity on people who won't do it voluntarily.
That's the actual function. The upsell is a bonus.

Which flips how you should read running out of likes. It isn't Tinder
being stingy. It's a warning that you're swiping in a pattern the app
reads as low-value, and the cap is the least expensive consequence of
that pattern.

## The number that actually matters

Here's the arithmetic nobody does.

Say you get 100 likes in 12 hours. If your profile converts at 2%, that
is two matches. At 10%, it's ten. The limit is identical in both cases.
The only variable you control is the rate.

Nobody runs out of likes because 100 is too few. They run out because
each like is worth very little, so they need a lot of them. Double your
conversion rate and 100 likes stops feeling like a constraint at all —
you'd get the same result from fifty.

This is the whole reason the limit is a bad thing to optimise around.
More swipes is the cheapest lever and the weakest one. Everything that
moves conversion is free and permanent.

**Fix the first photo.** It decides most of your swipes before anyone
reads a word.

**Fix the bio.** A bio doesn't win someone over on its own, but a bad

**Swipe like the likes are scarce.** They are. Being selective is
better for your match rate, better for how the algorithm ranks you, and
it means the cap stops being something you ever meet.

## The short version

Free Tinder gives you something like 100 likes per 12 hours. The exact
number varies by account, age and country, Tinder won't confirm it, and
the clock resets 12 hours after you run out rather than at a fixed
time. Super Likes are separate. Every paid tier makes likes unlimited.

But hitting the limit is a symptom, not a problem. It means you're
spending a lot of likes to get very little back, and the fix is on the
profile, not in the swipe budget. Get the conversion rate up and 100
likes a day is more than you'll ever need.
