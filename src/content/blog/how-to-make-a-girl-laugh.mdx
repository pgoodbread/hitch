Most advice on how to make a girl laugh is a list of jokes, which is
the one thing that reliably doesn't work. She has seen the jokes. The
guys who are funny in a conversation are almost never the ones with
better material — they're the ones who stopped treating the
conversation as a stage.

That's the useful framing here. You are probably not short on humour.
You're losing the jokes you already have to three or four habits that
are easy to name and not that hard to drop.

## Why "just be funnier" is useless advice

Funny isn't a trait you deploy. It's a thing that happens between two
people when one of them says something slightly unexpected about a
thing they're both already looking at.

Read that again, because the whole method is in it. Unexpected, about
something shared, said lightly. A joke you wrote in the shower this
morning fails the second condition, which is why it lands flat no
matter how good it was in the shower.

This is also why the funniest guy in your group chat can go completely
dead in a Tinder thread. In the group chat he's reacting. In the thread
he's producing. Producing is a much harder job and it shows.

## How to make a girl laugh without performing

Four things generate most of the laughs in a new conversation. None of
them require you to be witty on demand.

### Make a wrong guess, confidently

The highest-yield move in early texting. You take something from her
profile or her last message and you state a conclusion about it that is
obviously a little too specific.

- "Okay, the hiking photo is a trap. You've been up one mountain and
  you're going to make it a personality for the rest of the year."
- "Bio says you're competitive. About what. I need to know what I'm
  agreeing to."
- "Four photos, three of them with the same group of women. I'm going
  to assume they have veto power over this conversation."

She laughs, then she corrects you. That correction is the actual prize
— you've made her the expert on herself and given her something easy to
say back. A question gets you an answer. A wrong guess gets you an
answer with energy behind it.

### Call back to something she said

A callback is the cheapest laugh available and almost nobody uses it,
because it requires having read her messages.

If she mentioned on Tuesday that her coffee order is absurd, then on
Thursday you send: "Ordered a coffee today and thought of you.
Regular. Black. Took nine seconds." That's not a joke by any normal
definition. It will land better than anything you could write from
scratch, because it proves you were paying attention and it's a
reference only the two of you have.

Every conversation builds its own small vocabulary within about ten
messages. Use it.

### Deliver a fake-serious verdict

Taking something trivial and treating it with enormous gravity is
reliable, and it works in text where tone doesn't carry, because the
mismatch does the work instead of your delivery.

- "Pineapple on pizza is a red flag and I'm legally required to report
  it."
- "You said 'lol' instead of 'haha'. I have questions about your
  upbringing."

Keep it to one line. The moment you explain that you're joking, you've
turned it into a bit, and bits performed over text read as effort.

### Notice something instead of joking about it

Often the funniest thing available is just the observation nobody said
out loud. "We've been talking for twenty minutes and neither of us has
admitted we're both procrastinating something." No punchline. It still
gets a laugh, because it's true and slightly exposed.

This one travels best into real life, where an actual joke can feel
like a lot and a shared observation feels like you're on the same team.

## Over text versus in person

Most of this plays out over text, so it's worth being specific about
what changes.

Over text you lose tone and timing, which are two thirds of what makes
something funny in a room. What you gain is editing. You can delete the
first version. Use that — the first draft of a funny text is almost
always too long, and length is what kills it. If the funny part arrives
in sentence three, sentences one and two already announced that a joke
was coming.

In person the trade reverses. You get tone, face and timing for free,
and you lose the edit. Which means in person the move is to say less
and react more. A raised eyebrow at the right moment outperforms a
prepared line, every time, and you can't prepare it anyway.

The common failure is importing text habits into a real conversation —
arriving with three stories you've told before, and waiting for your
turn to tell them instead of listening. If you want the raw material
side of this, [what to talk about with a girl](/blog/what-to-talk-about-with-a-girl)
covers where the shared subjects come from. Jokes need something to be
about.

## The five habits that kill it

**Stacking.** The first one got no reply, so you send another. Now two
unanswered jokes are sitting there and the third will be read with real
hostility. One joke, then wait.

**Needing the laugh.** "😂😂" after your own line, "was that too much,"
"no but seriously." These all say the same thing: please confirm I'm
funny. Not caring whether it landed is most of what makes it land.

**Canned lines.** Borrowed material has a smell — too polished for the
thread it's in. [Funny pick up lines](/blog/funny-pick-up-lines) are
useful as a shape to copy, not a script to send, and the same rule
applies one step earlier in your bio.

**Punching down.** Teasing her about something she chose is fine.
Teasing her about something she didn't — her job, her height, her face
— isn't teasing. The test is whether she can plausibly hit back.

**Going funny-only.** If every message is a bid for a laugh, none of
them are. Humour works by contrast. A thread needs the other register
too, and a [flirty text](/blog/flirty-texts-for-her) does a job that
jokes can't.

## Tell whether it actually landed

The reply is usually legible, and guessing wrong here costs more than
the joke was worth.

She adds to it: you're in, keep the pace. She laughs and asks you
something: better, she's taken over steering. She laughs and stops:
polite, not engaged — change register and ask about her. A reaction
emoji with no words: cooling off, give it a day.

The ambiguous ones are where guys invent stories. A trailing ellipsis
[means something specific](/blog/meaning-in-text-from-a-girl), and it's
rarely the generous reading. A reply twelve hours later is information
about interest, not a puzzle about her schedule.

When it is going well — fast replies, she's building on your jokes,
the thread has its own running references — that's the window. Humour
got you there and it won't carry you further on its own, so
[ask her out](/blog/ask-girl-out-tinder) while the thread is still
warm. More jokes at that point are just a way of not asking.

## The short version

How to make a girl laugh comes down to reacting instead of performing.
Make a confident wrong guess she can correct. Call back to something
she said. Treat something trivial with total seriousness. Say the thing
you both noticed.

Then send it once and stop. The restraint after the line is doing as
much work as the line, and it's the part almost nobody gets right. If
you want more worked examples of the shapes above, the
[funny texts](/blog/funny-texts) breakdown goes situation by situation.
