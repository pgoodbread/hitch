Your matches stopped. Not slowed down — stopped. You're swiping the
same amount you always did, the app looks completely normal, and
nothing has come back for a week. Somewhere around day four you typed
"tinder shadowban" into Google, which is probably how you got here.

Here's the thing I'm going to be straight about up front: a real
shadowban exists, it's rarer than the internet suggests, and most
people who think they have one are watching something far more
ordinary happen to their account. Both of those are worth taking
seriously, because the fix is completely different depending on which
one you've got.

## What a Tinder shadowban actually is

A shadowban is when the app keeps working for you but stops working
for everyone else. Your profile loads, your swipes register, your old
matches are still there — you just don't appear in anyone's stack.
You're talking into a room that nobody is in.

Tinder has never confirmed that it does this, and it never will,
because the whole point of a quiet restriction is that it's quiet. But
enough accounts have reported the same pattern — full functionality,
zero inbound — that it's fair to treat it as real.

What Tinder does confirm is the loud version. Break the community
guidelines badly enough and you get banned outright, usually with error
code 40303, and the app tells you. That's not a shadowban. That's a
ban, and it's the one thing on this list you can't fix with better
photos.

## The three things that look identical from the inside

From your side of the screen, three very different situations produce
the exact same experience.

**An actual restriction.** Reports from other users, a flagged photo,
payment chargebacks, suspected bot behaviour, or a pattern of messages
that got you reported. The account stays usable, the reach goes to
zero.

**Review limbo.** If something on your account got flagged, it can sit
in a queue waiting on a human. During that window you're effectively
invisible. This resolves on its own and people rarely realise it
happened.

**Ordinary visibility decay.** This is the big one, and it's what's
actually happening to most people reading this. Tinder shows your
profile to a sample of people. If very few of them swipe right, it
shows you to fewer people, and then fewer again. Nobody decided
anything about you. The system just stopped spending impressions on a
profile that wasn't converting them.

The third one feels exactly like the first one. That's the trap. It
feels like a punishment because the drop is sudden, and a sudden drop
looks deliberate.

## How to tell which one you have

Run these in order. They take about ten minutes and they settle it.

**Check for an error message.** Open the app, log out, log back in. A
hard ban announces itself — 40303, or a message about violating terms.
No message means you're not hard banned. Stop worrying about that one.

**Check whether you're still consuming likes.** Swipe right thirty or
forty times on a free account and see if you hit the daily like cap.
Hitting the cap means the app is still counting your swipes as real
swipes.

**Get a second pair of eyes.** Have a friend set their filters to your
age and a tight distance radius around you, then swipe for a while and
see if you turn up. Not conclusive — the deck is big and you might
just not surface — but if they swipe for twenty minutes across two
sessions and you never appear, that's a signal.

**Look at the timeline.** Write down what changed in the week before
matches dried up. New photos? A bio rewrite? Did you get reported after
a conversation went badly? Did you use a third-party swipe automation
tool? Did you charge back a subscription? A shadowban usually has a
cause sitting right there in the last seven days. Visibility decay
doesn't — it just creeps.

**Count the last thirty days, not the last three.** Matches are lumpy.
Four dead days after a good week is normal variance, not a verdict.

If nothing changed, there's no error message, and the decline has been
gradual rather than a cliff, you are almost certainly in the third
bucket. That's the good news, even though it doesn't feel like it — it
means the thing holding you back is something you control.

## Why you probably aren't shadowbanned

The uncomfortable version: a shadowban is a satisfying explanation
because it's external. It says the problem is Tinder's, not yours, and
that nothing about your profile needs to change.

Visibility decay says the opposite. It says a few hundred people saw
you and kept scrolling, and the app drew the obvious conclusion. That's
a harder thing to sit with, which is exactly why "shadowban" is the
first search people make.

The mechanics aren't mysterious. Every profile shown to you is a
profile being tested on you, and
[your left swipes are data](/blog/swipe-left-meaning) in the same way
everyone else's are. A profile that gets swiped past consistently gets
shown less. There's no committee. It's a feedback loop, and it runs in
both directions — which is the part worth holding onto, because a
profile that starts converting climbs back out of the hole the same way
it fell in.

## If it is a real restriction

Assume your account is genuinely limited. The options are short.

**Wait it out.** Review limbo and soft restrictions often lift on their
own in one to two weeks. Keep using the app normally. Don't delete it.

**Contact support.** Tinder's support is slow and formulaic, but a
polite, specific message — what happened, when, what you think was
flagged — occasionally gets a real answer. Vent at them and you'll get
a template.

**Don't make a burner account.** This is the advice everyone gives and
it's usually wrong. Tinder ties accounts to your phone number, device
and payment details. A new account on the same phone tends to inherit
the problem, and evading a ban is itself a violation. If you were
banned outright, a second account is against the rules and generally
doesn't last.

**Stop doing whatever triggered it.** Automation tools, copy-pasted
opening messages sent at volume, and anything that gets you reported
are the usual causes. That behaviour will retrigger it on any account
you make.

## The fix that applies either way

Here's why the diagnosis matters less than it seems. If you're
restricted, you wait — and the smartest thing to do while waiting is
rebuild the profile, so you come back strong instead of returning to
the same dry spell. If you're not restricted, rebuilding the profile is
the entire fix. Both roads end in the same place.

Start with the first photo, because it's the only one most people ever
look at. It decides your swipe rate more than everything else combined,
and swapping a weak opener for a strong one is the single highest-value
change available to you.
[That one photo carries the whole profile](/blog/why-your-first-tinder-photo-decides-everything).

Then go structural rather than creative. Working from a
[profile template](/blog/tinder-profile-template) — a fixed photo order
with a specific job for each slot, and a bio in three lines — gets you
a competent profile in an afternoon without needing inspiration to
strike. The bio is the cheapest thing on the list to change and
[a bio that actually does something](/blog/best-tinder-bios-for-guys)
takes about twenty minutes.

What I'd skip is buying your way out. The reflex when matches stop is
to upgrade, and
[Tinder Gold multiplies your existing rate rather than fixing it](/blog/is-tinder-gold-worth-it).
If the rate is near zero, paying for three times the reach buys you
three times more nothing. Fix the conversion first, then decide whether
volume is worth money.

## If you're getting matches but no replies

Worth separating, because people file this under "shadowban" too. If
matches are landing and then dying in the chat, nothing is restricted —
your profile is working fine and the leak is downstream of it.

That's a messaging problem, and it's a much easier one to fix.
[Openers that get replies](/blog/tinder-openers) are mostly a matter of
giving her something specific to respond to instead of "hey". And
before you read silence as rejection, know that
[Tinder doesn't show read receipts by default](/blog/does-tinder-have-read-receipts),
so an unanswered message usually means she hasn't opened the app, not
that she's ignoring you.

## The short version

A Tinder shadowban is real but uncommon. Check for an error message,
check whether your swipes still count, have a friend look for you, and
look at what changed in the week before things went quiet. If there's a
clear trigger, you might be restricted — wait it out, contact support,
and don't make a burner.

If nothing changed and the decline was gradual, you're not banned.
You're being shown to fewer people because the people you were shown to
kept scrolling. That's fixable, it's fixable this afternoon, and it
starts with the first photo.
