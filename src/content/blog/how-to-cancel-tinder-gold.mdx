You subscribed, you gave it a fair run, and the matches didn't
arrive. Now you want out. The frustrating part is that how to cancel
Tinder Gold has almost nothing to do with Tinder — the subscription
lives with whoever took your money, which is usually Apple or Google,
and the app itself has no button that ends it.

That catches a lot of people out. They delete the app, assume it's
handled, and get charged again a month later.

Here are the three paths, depending on where you signed up, plus what
actually happens to your account afterwards.

## First, find out who is billing you

Tinder sells Gold through whatever platform you were on when you
tapped subscribe:

- Subscribed in the iPhone app → **Apple** is billing you
- Subscribed in the Android app → **Google Play** is billing you
- Subscribed at tinder.com in a browser → **Tinder** is billing you
  directly, through whatever card you entered

If you genuinely can't remember, check your bank statement. A charge
reading `APPLE.COM/BILL` means Apple, a charge from `GOOGLE *Tinder`
means Google Play, and anything naming Tinder or Match Group directly
means the web subscription.

You cancel in that place and nowhere else.

## How to cancel Tinder Gold on iPhone

Apple keeps every subscription in one screen, and it's not inside the
Tinder app.

1. Open **Settings** on your iPhone
2. Tap your **name** at the very top
3. Tap **Subscriptions**
4. Tap **Tinder** in the list
5. Tap **Cancel Subscription**, then confirm

If Tinder isn't in that list, you didn't subscribe through Apple —
try the Google or web instructions below. It's also worth checking
you're signed in with the same Apple ID you used at the time, because
a second Apple ID is the usual reason a subscription seems to vanish.

You can also get there from the App Store: tap your profile picture
in the top corner, then **Subscriptions**.

## How to cancel Tinder Gold on Android

Google Play works the same way — the subscription is in the store,
not the app.

1. Open the **Google Play Store**
2. Tap your **profile icon** in the top right
3. Tap **Payments and subscriptions**, then **Subscriptions**
4. Select **Tinder**
5. Tap **Cancel subscription** and pick a reason when asked

Google will ask why you're leaving and may offer you a pause instead.
A pause is not a cancellation. If you want it gone, decline the offer
and finish the flow until the subscription shows an end date.

Same caveat as Apple: make sure you're in the Google account you
subscribed with.

## How to cancel a web subscription at tinder.com

If you subscribed in a browser, Tinder bills you directly and you
cancel on the site.

1. Go to **tinder.com** and log in
2. Click your **profile icon**, then **Settings**
3. Find the **Manage Payment Account** or subscription section
4. Choose **Cancel Subscription** and confirm

This is the only route where Tinder handles the cancellation itself,
which is also why it's the only route where Tinder support can help
you directly if something goes wrong.

## Deleting the app doesn't cancel anything

This is the mistake that costs people money, so it's worth being
blunt about it.

Deleting the Tinder app removes it from your phone. It does not touch
your subscription, because the subscription is an agreement between
you and the app store, not between you and the icon on your home
screen.

Deleting your Tinder account doesn't cancel it either. You can wipe
the account entirely and Apple will keep billing you on schedule,
because nobody told Apple to stop.

Cancel the subscription first. Then, if you want to leave properly,
delete the account — in that order.

## What happens after you cancel

Nothing immediate, which surprises people who expect to be cut off
the moment they confirm.

**Gold runs until the end of the period you paid for.** If you cancel
on the 3rd and your month runs to the 20th, you keep See Who Likes
You, unlimited likes and everything else until the 20th. You are not
refunded the remainder, and you're not cut off early, so there's no
reason to wait until the last day to cancel.

**Your account reverts to free.** It is not deleted, suspended or
penalised. You keep your profile, your photos, your matches and your
entire message history. Swipes go back to the daily cap and the likes
grid goes blurry again.

**Your monthly Boost disappears.** Any unused Super Likes and the
Gold Boost go with the subscription, so spend them before the period
ends if you were saving them.

**Cancelling does not hurt your visibility.** There's a persistent
worry that the algorithm punishes people who stop paying. It doesn't
work like that — [how Tinder actually ranks
profiles](/blog/tinder-algorithm) has to do with how people respond to
you, not with your billing status. You lose the paid boost to reach,
and that's the whole of it.

## Refunds are handled by the store, not Tinder

Tinder's own policy is that payments are non-refundable, and support
will tell you the same. But the store that billed you has its own
rules, and they're more generous.

On iPhone, Apple has a **Report a Problem** page where you can request
a refund for a recent purchase. Apple decides case by case, and
accidental renewals within the last few days have a reasonable hit
rate.

On Android, Google Play has a refund request flow in your order
history, with a similar discretionary review.

Neither is guaranteed and neither is worth a long fight. Cancel first
so the next charge can't land, then ask about a refund — in that
order, because a pending refund request doesn't stop the renewal.

Some regions have consumer law that gives you stronger cancellation
rights than the app store's default, so if you're outside the US it's
worth checking what applies where you live.

## Check that it actually went through

Don't trust the confirmation screen on its own. Go back into the same
screen a day later:

- **iPhone:** Settings → your name → Subscriptions. Tinder should now
  appear under Expired, or show "Expires on [date]" rather than
  "Renews on [date]"
- **Android:** Play Store → Subscriptions. Tinder should show a
  cancelled status with an end date
- **Web:** the subscription section in Settings should no longer show
  an upcoming renewal

The word to look for is **expires**. If it still says renews, the
cancellation didn't take and you need to run through it again.

## What to fix instead

Most people cancelling Gold aren't cancelling because it stopped
working. They're cancelling because it never did what they hoped, and
that's worth being honest about before you write off the whole app.

Gold sells visibility. It shows your profile to more people and tells
you who already liked you. What it can't do is change how those
people react when they see you, and if the match rate was near zero
before you paid, more impressions of the same profile just produced
more left swipes. That's the trade the sales screen never mentions,
and it's the same reason [Boost tends to
disappoint](/blog/tinder-boost) for the people who need it most. If
you're still weighing up whether the money was wasted, the [full
case for and against Gold](/blog/is-tinder-gold-worth-it) is worth
ten minutes.

The lever that actually moves is the profile, and it's free.

Your first photo does more work than every paid feature combined,
because it's the only image most people ever see before deciding.
[Getting the photo selection right](/blog/profile-pictures) changes
the rate that everything else multiplies. After that, [a bio that
does a job](/blog/best-tinder-bios-for-guys) rather than listing
hobbies is twenty minutes of work and the second biggest lever you
have.

None of this costs anything. And if you fix it and later decide you
want more reach, resubscribing takes thirty seconds — this time
multiplying something worth multiplying. If the mechanics still feel
opaque, it helps to understand [what a left swipe actually
signals](/blog/swipe-left-meaning) on the other side.

## The short version

How to cancel Tinder Gold depends entirely on who billed you: Apple
through iPhone Settings, Google through the Play Store, or Tinder
directly if you subscribed on the web. Deleting the app cancels
nothing, and neither does deleting your account.

You keep Gold until the paid period ends, then drop to a free account
with your matches and messages intact. Refunds go through the store,
not Tinder, and only if you ask promptly.

Then check the screen again a day later and make sure it says expires
rather than renews. That's the part people skip, and it's the part
that stops the next charge.
