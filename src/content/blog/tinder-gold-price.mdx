You tapped the gold banner, saw a number, and now you want to know
whether that number is normal. Fair question, and an annoying one to
answer, because there is no single Tinder Gold price. Tinder sets it
per account, and the figure on your screen is probably not the figure
on your friend's.

Here's the realistic range, the four things that move it, and the
cheapest way to pay if you decide to.

## What the Tinder Gold price actually is

In the US, paying month to month, Gold usually lands somewhere in the
$30 to $40 range. Published figures through 2025 and 2026 have put the
list price around $39.99 a month, with plenty of accounts seeing less.

Commit to a longer term and the monthly rate drops hard — a six-month
plan typically works out near $23 to $25 a month, and twelve months
goes lower still. That's a real discount, not a rounding error, which
is exactly why the app pushes the longer terms first.

Outside the US the numbers shift with local pricing, and in some
markets they shift a lot.

Treat all of that as a sanity check, not a quote. If the app is showing
you something inside that band, you're being charged a normal price. If
it's showing you something far outside it, read the next section.

## Why your price is different from everyone else's

Four things move the number, and only one of them is in your control.

**Your age.** This is the big one. Tinder has consistently charged
older users more for the same tier — the split usually sits around 30.
Same features, same app, different bill. It's legal in most places and
it's been the subject of lawsuits in a few others, but as a buyer the
only thing that matters is that it's real and you can't opt out of it.

**Your country.** Pricing is set per market. If you've seen a wildly
lower number quoted in a forum, check which country the person posting
is in before you conclude you're being overcharged.

**Your term.** Month to month is the most expensive way to pay by a
wide margin. Six and twelve month plans cut the effective monthly rate
by a third to a half.

**Testing.** Tinder runs pricing experiments more or less constantly,
so two accounts that look identical can land in different test groups.
There's no trick to getting into the cheaper one.

That's also why no article, including this one, can tell you your
price. Open the app, hit the subscribe screen, and read what it says.
That's your number.

## Gold vs Plus vs Platinum, by what you pay for

The tiers are easier to judge as a ladder than as a feature list.

**Plus** is the cheap tier, usually in the $20 to $25 range month to
month. Unlimited likes, Rewind, Passport, no ads. It does **not** show
you who liked you. If your only problem is hitting the daily swipe cap,
this is the tier that solves it, and you'll save real money over Gold.

**Gold** is Plus plus See Who Likes You, a monthly
[Boost](/blog/tinder-boost), and a weekly Super Like allowance. The
likes grid is the whole reason anyone steps up from Plus. Everything
else in the jump is garnish.

**Platinum** usually runs around $10 more than Gold and adds Priority
Likes and Message Before Match. Those only help if your message and
your photos are already good, which makes it the tier to buy last.

**Select** exists, costs several hundred a month, and is invite-only.
If you're reading an article about pricing, it isn't for you.

The gap worth thinking about is Plus to Gold. You're paying roughly
$15 a month extra for one feature: knowing who already liked you.
Whether that's worth it depends entirely on whether there's anyone in
that grid, which depends on your profile rather than your card.

## Where you pay changes what you pay

Most people subscribe inside the app, which means Apple or Google takes
a cut and the price reflects it. Subscribing at tinder.com in a browser
sometimes comes in cheaper, because that cut isn't in the middle.

It's worth two minutes to compare. Open the subscribe screen in the
app, note the number, then log in on the web and look at the same tier.
If they match, pay wherever you like. If the web is cheaper, pay there.

One practical catch: whoever takes your money is also the only one who
can stop taking it. A web subscription is cancelled with Tinder, an
iPhone subscription with Apple, an Android one with Google — and
[deleting the app cancels nothing](/blog/how-to-cancel-tinder-gold).
Know which one you signed up through before you need to get out.

## The things that aren't included

A few features people assume are part of the Gold price are sold
separately, as one-off credits rather than subscription perks.

**Extra Boosts and Super Boosts.** Gold includes one Boost a month.
More than that, you buy.

**Super Likes beyond your weekly allowance.** Same deal.

**Read receipts.** These are credits, not a tier feature, and
[they're a weaker purchase than they look](/blog/does-tinder-have-read-receipts).

The credits are where a $40 subscription quietly becomes a $70 month.
If you subscribe, decide up front that the allowance is the budget.

## Free trials and the discount screens

Tinder runs promotional pricing fairly often, usually as a discount on
the first term rather than a true free trial. You'll also see a
discount appear if you open the subscribe screen, back out, and come
back later — a standard retention nudge.

None of this is a reason to buy. It's a reason to not buy in the first
thirty seconds. If you're on the fence, closing the screen and checking
again in a few days costs you nothing and sometimes costs you less.

Watch the renewal term, too. The discount almost always applies to the
first period only, and the plan renews at full price unless you cancel.
A cheap six months becomes an expensive twelve if you forget.

## Is the price the right question?

Not really, and this is the part the subscribe screen is designed to
keep you from thinking about.

Every paid tier is a multiplier on the match rate you already have. If
100 people see your profile and two swipe right, paying to be seen by
300 gets you six matches. If your rate is close to zero, three times
more impressions of the same profile gets you three times more nothing.
You've bought a bigger audience for the same result.

So before you compare the Tinder Gold price against the Platinum price,
work out which problem you have.
[If matches aren't happening at all](/blog/why-am-i-not-getting-matches-on-tinder),
the bottleneck is conversion, and no tier fixes conversion. Your first
photo and
[a bio that does some work](/blog/best-tinder-bios-for-guys) move that
number for free, permanently, in an afternoon.

If your profile already converts and you simply want more volume or
want to stop spending an hour a night swiping, then paying for reach
makes sense — and
[the full case for and against Gold](/blog/is-tinder-gold-worth-it)
is worth reading before you commit to a year of it.

## The short version

Expect roughly $30 to $40 a month for Tinder Gold in the US paying
month to month, dropping toward $23 to $25 on a six-month term and
lower on twelve. Your age, your country, your term, and whichever
pricing test you landed in decide where in that band you sit.

Check the web price against the app price before you pay. Budget for
the subscription only, not the credits. And buy a month with a specific
reason attached rather than a year out of frustration — the profile is
the thing you keep either way.
