The dating questions to ask once you are sitting across from someone
are not the ones you used over text. Texting questions exist to prove
the conversation is worth having. Date questions exist to find out
whether the person is worth seeing again — and to let them find out
the same about you.

Most question lists ignore that difference. They hand you a hundred
prompts in no order, half of them fine for a chat and half of them
things you would only ask after six months. On a date that is worse
than useless, because the wrong question at the wrong moment is
audible.

Everything below is grouped by what the question is for and what the
answer actually tells you.

## What makes a date question different from a text question

Over text you are optimising for a reply. In person you already have
their attention for two hours, so the job changes. You are now
optimising for a conversation that goes somewhere on its own.

Three things change with it.

- **Length is allowed.** A question that takes two minutes to answer
  is a gift on a date and a chore over text. You can finally ask the
  ones with stories attached.
- **Reactions do half the work.** You can see them think. A question
  that lands shows on their face, which means you can follow the
  interesting thread instead of guessing.
- **Silence is fine.** Three seconds of thinking over text reads as
  being left on read. Across a table it reads as someone taking the
  question seriously.

If you are still at the stage before any of this — the chat has not
started, or it has not reached a date yet — those are different
problems. [Tinder openers](/blog/tinder-openers) covers the first
message, and [questions to ask your
crush](/blog/question-to-ask-your-crush) covers the stretch between
matching and meeting.

## First-date questions that open things up

The first twenty minutes have one job: get both of you talking like
people instead of interviewees. Ask things that are easy to start
answering and hard to finish in one sentence.

> What have you been spending too much time on lately?

> What is something you have changed your mind about this year?

> Best thing you have eaten in the last month. Be specific.

> What were you like at sixteen? How much of that is still in there?

> What is the most unnecessary thing you own and genuinely love?

The pattern is the same in all of them. They are concrete, they have
an obvious first sentence, and none of them could be asked by a form.
"What do you do?" gets you a job title. "What is the part of your job
you would keep if you quit tomorrow?" gets you a person.

One thing worth doing early: ask about something that happened today.
It is low stakes, it is shared ground, and it resets the conversation
from audition to hangout.

## Dating questions to ask that reveal values

Somewhere in the middle of a good date you stop collecting facts and
start working out whether you actually fit. These are the dating
questions to ask for that, and the trick is that none of them sound
heavy.

> How do you like to spend a Sunday when nothing is planned?

> Who do you text first when something good happens?

> What is something everyone around you seems to enjoy that you just
> do not get?

> Are you someone who makes plans or someone who gets invited to
> them?

> What would you do with a completely free year?

Each one answers a question you cannot ask directly. The Sunday
question tells you their default speed. The "who do you text first"
question tells you who is actually close to them. The free year
question tells you whether their ambitions are about building
something, seeing something, or being left alone — all fine answers,
but you want to know which one you are dealing with.

Listen for the shape of the answer as much as the content. Someone who
answers fast and specifically has thought about it. Someone who
deflects to a joke every time is telling you something too.

## Questions that build attraction

Attraction on a date comes from the conversation getting mutual, not
from clever lines. Questions build it when they invite the other
person to be interesting rather than to be assessed.

> What is the fastest way someone could impress you, and how obvious
> is it when they are trying?

> What do people get wrong about you when they first meet you?

> What is a thing you are weirdly competitive about?

> If this date went badly, what is the most likely reason?

That last one is the best one on the list. It is funny, it
acknowledges the situation you are both in, and the answer is almost
always honest. People relax when you admit out loud that a first date
is a strange thing to be doing.

Keep the flirting pointed at who they are rather than what they look
like. A compliment about their taste, their job, or the way they tell
a story invites more conversation. A compliment about their body ends
it. The backhanded version — the "you are cute for someone who…"
school — reads as a warning sign now, and
[why negging backfires](/blog/negging) is worth reading if anyone has
sold you on it.

## Second and third date questions

By the second date the icebreakers are spent and the real filtering
starts. You can go further because you have earned it.

> What does a good week look like for you right now?

> What is something you want in the next couple of years that you
> have not told many people about?

> How did your last relationship end, and what did you take from it?

> What do you need from someone when you are having a bad week?

The last two sound like a lot. They are not, by date two or three,
and the answers matter more than anything you learn in the first
hour. Someone who can describe what went wrong without making the
other person a villain is telling you how they handle conflict.
Someone who needs space when things are hard, said out loud, is
saving you both a fight in three months.

Ask one of these per date, not all four. They work because they
arrive in the middle of a normal conversation, not because they are
deep.

## How to ask without it turning into an interview

Two questions in a row is a conversation. Four is an interrogation,
and people answer interrogations briefly and then go home.

The fix costs one sentence: answer your own question first, or react
to what they said before you ask the next thing. "What is the most
unnecessary thing you own?" followed by "mine is a sandwich toaster I
have used twice" turns a prompt into an exchange. You have given them
something to react to instead of a second task.

Follow-ups beat new questions every time. If they mention a trip,
ask about the trip. The best question on any date is almost always
the one built out of the sentence they just said — no list required.
When the thread genuinely runs out, [what to talk about with a
girl](/blog/what-to-talk-about-with-a-girl) goes further on restarting
one without reaching for a new question.

## Questions to avoid

- **The CV round.** Job, hometown, siblings, university, in sequence.
  It is the fastest way to make a date feel like onboarding.
- **Anything with a right answer.** "Do you want kids?" on date one is
  not curiosity, it is a screening form, and people answer it
  defensively.
- **Body count and exes, early.** There is no version of this that
  improves the night.
- **Yes/no questions.** Not wrong, just dead ends. Put "what" or
  "how" in front of the same question and it works.
- **Questions you do not care about the answer to.** The tell is that
  you never follow up on what they said, and it is extremely
  visible in person.

## What the answers are telling you

Pay attention to the ratio. On a date that is going well, questions
go both ways within the first half hour. If you have asked eight and
been asked none, the questions are not the problem — the interest
level is, and more questions will not fix it.

Short answers with nothing handed back is the same signal. Try giving
more than you ask for a couple of times. If it stays one-directional
after that, you have your answer, and it is a cheap one to get on
date one rather than date four.

## The takeaway

The dating questions to ask are the ones that are concrete, open-ended,
and pitched at the stage you are actually at. Early on, ask things
with stories attached. In the middle, ask the ones that quietly reveal
how someone lives. By date two, ask what they need and how things have
gone wrong before. Then stop asking and follow the thread.
