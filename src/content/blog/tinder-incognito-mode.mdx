Tinder Incognito Mode hides your profile from everyone except the
people you have already swiped right on. You keep swiping normally.
Nobody else sees you in their stack. Most people find it while looking
for a way to use the app without a coworker, a cousin, or an ex
running into them at two in the morning.

It does that job well. The part worth understanding before you switch
it on is what it costs you in matches, because the feature works by
removing you from the one place matches come from.

## What Tinder Incognito Mode actually does

With Incognito Mode on, you disappear from the card stack. People
swiping in your area do not see you, cannot like you, and have no way
to reach you. From their side you are simply not on Tinder.

The exception is the thing that makes the feature usable: if you swipe
right on someone, you become visible to them. Your card drops into
their stack as normal, and if they swipe right back, you match. So the
direction of discovery flips. Instead of everyone seeing you and you
reacting to who liked you, you see everyone and only the people you
choose see you.

Three things do not change:

**Your existing matches stay put.** Every conversation you already
have stays open. The people in your match list can still message you,
and you can still message them. Incognito only governs who can find
you in the stack.

**Nobody is told.** There is no notification, no badge, no "this user
is browsing privately" marker. To someone who swiped right on you last
month and never heard back, your silence looks identical to being
ignored.

**You can turn it off whenever.** It is a toggle, not a one-way door.
Flip it back and you return to the stack.

## Incognito Mode vs the old "Show me on Tinder" toggle

Tinder has carried a hide-me switch for years. The old version lived
in settings as "Show me on Tinder" and did roughly the same thing when
you turned it off: you vanished from the stack while keeping your
matches.

Incognito Mode is that idea made explicit and moved behind a paid
tier. If you are looking for the free toggle you remember, that is
what happened to it. The practical difference for you is the price,
not the behaviour.

It is also worth separating this from Tinder's other privacy controls,
because people lump them together. Incognito controls who sees your
profile at all. [Changing your display name](/blog/how-to-change-name-on-tinder)
controls what they see when they do. And
[read receipts](/blog/does-tinder-have-read-receipts) are about
visibility in the other direction — what you get to see about them.
Three separate settings, three separate questions.

## What Incognito Mode costs

It is a subscriber feature. It ships with Tinder Plus, Gold, and
Platinum, and there is no way to buy it on its own.

Which tiers include it, and what each tier costs, moves around by
country and by promotion often enough that any number printed in an
article goes stale. Open the app and read the current offer rather
than trusting a figure you found online, this one included.

The decision in front of you is therefore not "is privacy worth a few
dollars" but "am I starting a subscription." Incognito is one line
item inside a bundle, and
[whether that bundle is worth it](/blog/is-tinder-gold-worth-it) is
its own question with its own answer. If you subscribe to try it and
decide the trade is bad,
[cancelling cleanly on each platform](/blog/how-to-cancel-tinder-gold)
takes about a minute, and it is worth doing properly rather than
deleting the app and assuming billing stops.

## How to turn Incognito Mode on and off

The toggle lives in your settings, not in the swiping screen.

1. Open Tinder and tap your profile icon.
2. Go to **Settings**.
3. Scroll to the **Control Who Sees You** section.
4. Turn **Incognito Mode** on.

If you are not subscribed, tapping it opens the upgrade screen instead
of switching anything on. Turning it off is the same path in reverse,
and the change takes effect immediately — you reappear in stacks
without any waiting period or review.

Menu wording shifts between app versions and platforms. If the section
heading does not match, look for whatever groups your visibility
settings together; the toggle has not moved out of settings.

## Who Incognito Mode is actually for

**You have a real privacy problem.** A small town, a public-facing
job, a family that would have opinions, an ex who checks. If being
seen on the app has a genuine cost, Incognito removes that cost for
the price of a subscription. That is a fair trade and the feature's
best use by a distance.

**You want to browse before committing.** Freshly single, or just
curious what the pool looks like, and not ready to be a visible
participant. Incognito lets you look around without announcing
anything.

**You only want people you actively chose.** Some people find an
inbox full of likes from people they would never swipe on more
draining than useful. Flipping the direction fixes that — every match
you get starts with a decision you made.

**It is not for fixing a quiet queue.** This is the case to rule out
hard. If the problem is that nothing is happening, hiding yourself
from everyone who has not already been chosen by you makes it happen
less.

## The tradeoff nobody mentions

Matches are impressions times your right-swipe rate. Incognito Mode
does nothing to the rate and cuts the impressions to whatever you
generate yourself, one swipe at a time.

Normally your profile is working while you are not. It sits in other
people's stacks all day, collecting likes from people who found you.
Switch Incognito on and that entire channel closes. Your reach becomes
exactly the number of people you personally swipe right on — and
[Tinder caps how many likes you get](/blog/how-many-likes-do-you-get-on-tinder),
so there is a hard ceiling on it.

For most people that is a large cut. Not a subtle one.

There is a second effect that is easy to miss. Because every match now
begins with your right swipe, your own swiping standards become the
only filter in the system. Swipe broadly and you are back to volume
with extra steps. Swipe carefully and your match count drops again.

None of this makes the feature bad. It makes it a privacy tool that
costs you reach, which is fine if privacy is what you came for, and
expensive if you came for matches.

The upside is that the people who do see you are a smaller, better
targeted group — which only pays off if your profile converts them.
With fewer impressions, each one has to work harder, so
[a bio built to land cold](/blog/best-tinder-bios-for-guys) matters
more under Incognito than it does in the open stack, not less.

## Incognito Mode is not a shadowban, and it is not a cure for one

These get confused constantly, in both directions.

A shadowban is something Tinder does to you without telling you:
your profile stops being shown, your likes stop landing, and nothing
in your settings explains it. Incognito is something you do on
purpose, visible in your own settings, reversible in one tap.

The confusion runs the other way too. People who suspect they have
been shadowbanned sometimes switch Incognito on and off hoping to
reset something. It resets nothing. The toggle changes one visibility
flag and touches nothing about your account standing.

If you turned Incognito on weeks ago, forgot, and have been wondering
why the app went quiet — check the toggle before you conclude anything
about the algorithm. That mistake is more common than it sounds.

## The short version

Tinder Incognito Mode hides you from everyone you have not swiped
right on, keeps your existing matches and conversations intact, comes
bundled with the paid tiers, and flips off as easily as it flips on.

Use it when being seen on Tinder carries a real cost. That is a
genuine problem and this is a genuine fix for it.

Do not use it to fix a slow queue. It works by shrinking the number of
people who can find you, which is the opposite of what a slow queue
needs. If matches are what you are short of, the first photo and the
bio are free to change and they move the number that Incognito cannot
touch.
