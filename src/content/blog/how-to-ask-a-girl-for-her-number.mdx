Nobody loses a match at the number. They lose it in the four days
before, while the conversation is going well enough that asking feels
like it would spoil something. Then she stops replying and the thread
sits there, perfectly pleasant, completely dead.

So the real question of how to ask a girl for her number is not which
words to use. It is when, and what the ask is actually for. Get those
two right and the phrasing almost does not matter. Get them wrong and
no line saves you.

## The number is not the goal

This trips people up, so it is worth being blunt about.

A phone number is not a win. It is a logistics step — a way of moving
a conversation somewhere that does not depend on her opening an app
full of other men. Plenty of guys collect numbers and never meet
anyone, because they treated the number as the finish line and then
had nothing to say once they crossed it.

The thing you are actually building toward is a drink on Thursday.
The number is how you get the Thursday organised. Hold that in your
head and the ask stops feeling like a big swing, because it is not
one. It is admin.

## When to ask

There is a window, and it is narrower than most men think.

Too early and you are asking a stranger to hand personal information
to another stranger on the basis of nothing. Too late and the thread
has settled into a pen-pal rhythm where asking would be a change of
gear she no longer expects.

The window opens when three things are true: the conversation has gone
back and forth enough that you are not strangers, she has given you at
least one answer longer than she needed to, and there is something
specific you could plausibly meet about. In practice that is usually
somewhere between message eight and message twenty — day one or day
two of a thread that is moving.

The clearest signal is reciprocity. She is asking you things. Not
politely returning your question with "you?" tacked on, but actually
asking. When a woman starts investing in the conversation, she has
decided it is going somewhere. Ask then.

The second signal is a hook. She mentions the climbing gym, or that
she has never been to the place on the corner, or that her week is
chaos until Friday. Any of those gives the ask a reason to exist,
which is what separates it from a cold request.

## How to ask a girl for her number

The phrasing that works does three things at once: it names a specific
plan, it gives a reason to swap apps, and it is easy to say yes to.

Here is the shape.

> **You:** I'm out Thursday evening and there's a wine bar near the
> station I keep meaning to try. Give me your number and I'll sort out
> a time with you properly

Three things are doing work there. **A specific plan** — Thursday, a
place, not "we should get a drink sometime." **A reason** — the number
is for sorting the details, which is obviously true and makes it feel
practical rather than personal. **An instruction, not a question** —
"give me your number" is easier to answer than "would it be okay if I
asked for your number," which invites her to evaluate the request
instead of the plan.

A softer version, same bones:

> **You:** This is a much better conversation than the app deserves.
> What's your number — I'll text you about Thursday

And the version where the hook does the work:

> **Her:** honestly I've lived here two years and never been
>
> **You:** That's criminal and I'm fixing it. Send me your number,
> I'll find us a Thursday

What none of those do is ask permission to ask. "Would it be weird if
I got your number?" tells her you think it might be weird, and she
will generally agree with you.

## The four ways guys lose it

**Asking with no plan attached.** "Can I get your number?" out of
nowhere is a request for her contact details with nothing offered in
return. She has to decide whether she trusts you, in the abstract,
with no upside on the table. Attach a plan and she is deciding about
the plan instead.

**Asking on message two.** The number ask is not an opener. If your
[opening message](/blog/good-pickup-lines-for-girls) is followed
immediately by a request for her number, you have told her you are
running a volume operation. Build something first — a couple of
exchanges with some [teasing](/blog/how-to-tease-a-girl-over-text) in
them is worth more than any phrasing you could use later.

**Dumping your own number instead.** "Here's mine, text me whenever"
feels polite and is a quiet way of making her do the work. She
usually will not. Ask for hers, and be the one who sends the first
message.

**Never asking at all.** The most common failure by a distance. A
thread with thirty good exchanges and no invitation is a loss that
felt like a win the whole way through. Threads do not get warmer
indefinitely — they peak, and then they cool. Ask near the peak.

## When she stalls

Not every answer is yes or no. Plenty are neither.

**"I'd rather just chat here for now."** Fine, and not a rejection.
She is saying the trust is not there yet, which is a reasonable thing
for a stranger to say. Take it at face value, carry on with the
conversation, and ask again in a day or two once there is more behind
it. Do not sulk, and do not explain that you are normal.

**She answers the plan but skips the number.** "Thursday could work!"
with no digits. She is interested in the date and not ready to move
apps. Run with it — pin down the time on the app, and the number
usually arrives on its own before Thursday.

**She goes quiet for a few hours.** Usually her day, not your ask. Do
not follow up with "no worries if not!!" at midnight, which withdraws
the invitation on her behalf. Leave it. If nothing by tomorrow
evening, send something unrelated and light, and let the ask stand.

**"Give me yours instead."** Say yes, send it, and then actually text
her first when she messages you. Some women prefer controlling who has
their number, which has nothing to do with you.

## When she says no

Sometimes it is a no. The only thing that matters is what you do in
the next sixty seconds.

> **You:** No worries at all. What's the verdict on the ketchup thing
> then, I'm still not over it

Acknowledge it in half a sentence and go straight back to the
conversation you were having. No apology, no explanation, no sudden
shift into being careful with her. A graceful no-reaction is the
single most attractive thing available to you in that moment, and
more than a few threads turn around a day later because of it.

What kills it is negotiating. "Is it something I said?" or "we can
just be friends haha" both drag her into managing your feelings about
a decision she just made. Let it go and keep being good company.

## Once you have it

Text her the same day. Not three days later — the delay game costs
more matches than it has ever won.

Open with something from the thread, not a reintroduction. "Hey it's
Mathew from Tinder :)" makes her do the work of remembering. A
callback does the opposite:

> **You:** Right, it's me. Wine bar's booked for 7 on Thursday,
> ketchup is banned on the premises, I checked

Then confirm the detail and stop texting into the void. The number
exists to get Thursday done, and over-texting between the ask and the
date is how a confirmed plan turns into a cancelled one. Keep it warm
with the occasional [flirty text](/blog/flirty-texts-for-her), keep it
light, and save the rest for the table.

If the date itself is the part you are unsure about, the
[questions you ask in person](/blog/dating-questions-to-ask) are a
different skill from the ones that worked over text, and worth
reading up on separately.

## The part that happens before the ask

Two men can send the identical message and get different answers, and
the difference is rarely the message.

If the ask lands badly over and over, the problem is usually upstream
of the thread. A profile that reads as generic produces conversations
that stay polite, and polite conversations do not survive a number
ask — there is nothing in them worth moving apps for. The threads
where the number comes easily are the ones with a bit of heat in
them, and that heat usually starts with a profile that gave her
something to react to.

Volume matters too, in a way that is hard to see from inside. When
matches arrive steadily, no individual thread is carrying your week,
and that lightness is audible. You ask earlier, you ask better, and a
no costs you nothing. When a match is scarce, every ask gets delayed
until the moment has passed. Fixing that is a photo problem far more
often than it is a texting problem.

## The short version

Ask once the conversation is genuinely two-way, attach a specific
plan, and phrase it as an instruction rather than a request. Do not
ask on message two, do not hand her your number instead, and do not
let a good thread run out of road while you wait for a better moment.

If she stalls, keep talking and ask again later. If she says no, let
it go in half a sentence and carry on. And when she does send it,
text her that day about Thursday — because that is what the number
was always for. The rest is just
[asking her out](/blog/ask-girl-out-tinder), which is the easy part
once you are off the app. Keep the thread [funny](/blog/how-to-make-a-girl-laugh)
on the way there and you will rarely need to ask twice.
