Tinder means dry, flammable material — the scraps of bark, straw and
wood shavings you light to start a fire from a spark. The app borrowed
the name in 2012 because the pitch was the same picture: a spark
between two people, then whatever they do with it.

That's the tinder meaning you want if you read the word on a label or
in a book. If you got here from the app, the more useful half of the
answer is the rest of this page — what the thing actually is, and what
every piece of its vocabulary means. Tinder generated its own dialect
and nobody hands you a glossary when you sign up.

## Tinder meaning, in one line

Two definitions, and they're connected.

**The word.** Tinder is light, dry material that catches fire easily.
It's old — Old English *tynder* — and it survives mostly in the phrase
"tinderbox" and in the sense of something ready to go up at the
slightest provocation.

**The app.** Tinder is a dating app that shows you one profile at a
time. Swipe right for yes, left for no. If two people swipe right on
each other, they match, and only then can either of them send a
message.

It's pronounced like the fire material, so it rhymes with "cinder,"
not with "finder." That's worth knowing only because plenty of people
get it wrong out loud.

## Where the name came from

Tinder launched in 2012 out of a startup incubator, and the metaphor
was deliberate: the app provides the kindling, you provide the fire.
Hence the flame logo.

The naming is less interesting than the mechanic it was attached to.
Before Tinder, online dating meant long profiles, search filters, and
messages from strangers you never agreed to hear from. Tinder replaced
all of it with a binary gesture and one rule — nobody can message you
unless you also said yes.

That rule is why the app spread. It killed the unsolicited-inbox
problem in a single design decision, and every major dating app has
some version of it now.

## The swipe is the whole app

Everything else is built on top of two gestures.

**Swipe right** means yes, I'm interested. The card disappears and your
like is recorded. Nothing visible happens next — it only becomes
something if she swipes right on you too. The full version, including
the daily limit on free accounts, is in
[swipe right meaning](/blog/swipe-right-meaning).

**Swipe left** means no. The card is gone and it doesn't come back
unless you pay for the undo. Left swipes are unlimited, which is a
deliberate asymmetry:
[swipe left meaning](/blog/swipe-left-meaning) covers why passing is
free and liking isn't.

Both phrases escaped the app years ago and now work as ordinary slang
for approving or rejecting anything — a job listing, a restaurant, a
Tuesday.

## Every Tinder term, translated

The rest of the vocabulary, in the order you'll run into it.

**Match.** Two people swiped right on each other. A chat opens and
either one can write first. A match is not a date, an obligation, or
even a conversation — it's two people who agreed to be in each other's
inbox. Most of them die there, unopened.

**Like.** A right swipe. Free accounts get a capped number in a
rolling window, generally around 100 per 12 hours, though Tinder
adjusts the number and has never published it.

**Super Like.** The blue star. It signals stronger interest and, unlike
a normal like, she's told about it before she decides — your card shows
up with a blue border. Free accounts get a small allowance, usually one
a day. Opinions on whether they help are split; they're unambiguously a
signal of effort, which cuts both ways.

**Rewind.** The undo for your most recent swipe. Paid only, so on a
free account an accidental left swipe is permanent.

**Boost.** Puts your profile near the top of the local stack for 30
minutes. It buys visibility, not interest — a boost on a weak profile
just shows a weak profile to more people, faster.

**Unmatch.** Either person can remove a match at any time. The chat
disappears for both of you with no notification and no way back.

**Ghosting.** Not a Tinder feature, just what happens: someone stops
replying without ending the conversation. It's the default failure mode
of app dating, and it's rarely about anything you can diagnose.

**Bio.** The text on your profile, up to 500 characters. Optional, and
leaving it blank costs you more than most guys think.

**Tinder Plus, Gold, Platinum.** The three paid tiers. Plus adds
unlimited likes, Rewind and Passport; Gold adds seeing who already
liked you; Platinum adds messaging before matching and priority on your
likes.

**Passport.** Swipe in another city as if you were there. Part of the
paid tiers.

**Shadowban.** Not an official feature, and Tinder doesn't confirm it
exists. The term describes an account that still works from the inside
while barely being shown to anyone — usually after mass right-swiping
or reports.

**Top Picks and Explore.** Curated stacks Tinder assembles for you,
by its own guess at your taste and by interest category.

## What the vocabulary doesn't tell you

Learn the words and the app sounds like a volume game: more swipes,
more likes, more matches. The mechanics underneath say the opposite.

Your right swipes are an input the app reads. Like everything and your
likes carry no information about what you want, so Tinder can't put you
in front of people likely to like you back. Worse, a right-swipe rate
of 400-to-3 tells the app that showing your profile mostly wastes
everyone's time — so it shows it less. The strategy meant to raise your
numbers lowers them.

The other thing the glossary hides: getting matches and getting replies
are separate problems with separate causes. Matching is decided in
about a second, from a thumbnail, before a word of your bio is read.
Replies are decided by what you send after. Fixing the wrong one is the
most common way to spend three months and get nowhere.

## The takeaway

Tinder means kindling: dry material that catches a spark. The app took
the name in 2012 for the obvious metaphor, and built the whole thing on
one gesture — right for yes, left for no, message only after both
people agree.

The vocabulary around it is small and worth knowing: match, like, super
like, boost, unmatch, the three paid tiers. What the words don't tell
you is the part that decides how the app goes for you. Your swipes are
a signal, not a lottery ticket, and the profile they're attached to
does almost all of the work.
