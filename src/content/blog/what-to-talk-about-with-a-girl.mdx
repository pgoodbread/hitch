Most guys do not run out of things to say on message one. They run out
on message four. The opener worked, she answered, and then the thread
turned into two people confirming that it is indeed Thursday.

What to talk about with a girl is really a question about direction. A
topic is not a sentence you send — it is a place the conversation can
go for the next ten messages without either of you working. Some topics
have that in them. Most of the ones guys reach for do not.

## Why the conversation dies at message four

The opener has a job and it does it: get a reply. Then the job changes,
and almost nobody changes with it.

What usually happens is interview mode. You ask where she is from. She
answers. You ask what she does. She answers. Every message closes a
loop instead of opening one, and after four rounds she is doing
paperwork on her phone with a stranger. She does not decide to stop
replying. She just stops having a reason to pick the phone back up.

The fix is not more questions. It is picking subjects that leave
something hanging — a take she can disagree with, a story she can top,
a claim about her she can correct.

## What to talk about with a girl: four topics that go somewhere

These are categories, not scripts. The point is to recognise the shape
so you can build your own out of whatever is in front of you.

**1. The thing she chose to include.** Her bio and photos are a list of
things she volunteered. That is not small talk — that is her telling
you what she wants to be asked about. The hiking photo, the line about
her terrible sense of direction, the prompt answer she clearly spent
time on. Same principle that drives a good
[Tinder opener](/blog/tinder-openers), except now you are going deeper
on one detail instead of picking a new one each time.

**2. Low-stakes opinions.** Ask her to rank, judge, or defend something
that does not matter. Best cheap meal in the city. Whether a hot dog is
a sandwich. Which of her friends would survive the longest in a
zombie situation. These work because there is no correct answer, so she
cannot fail, and because disagreement is fun when nothing is at stake.

**3. Her actual week, but specific.** "How was your day" is dead on
arrival. "How bad was the Monday" is not, and neither is asking about
the exact thing she mentioned dreading three messages ago. Specific
questions get specific answers, and specific answers give you something
to react to. There is a longer set of these in the
[questions to ask your crush](/blog/question-to-ask-your-crush) that
work the same way here.

**4. Playful accusations.** Notice a pattern and name it. She has
mentioned three coffee shops in ten minutes, so she is clearly running
a caffeine operation. She said she is competitive, so you assume she
cheats at board games. This gives her the easiest reply in texting —
defending herself — and it turns the thread into a bit you both share
rather than a form she is filling in.

## Topics that stall

Some subjects are not neutral. They actively cost you momentum.

**Work, past the first exchange.** Most people do not want to think
about their job at 9pm. One question is fine. A follow-up about her
industry is a wet blanket.

**Compliments about her looks, repeatedly.** She knows. It also
collapses the conversation into a single dimension, and then there is
nowhere left to go that is not more of the same.

**Anything answerable in one word.** If "yeah" completes it, you have
handed her an exit. Check your message before you send: could she end
this with a single syllable and not be rude? Rewrite it.

**Heavy stuff, early.** Exes, family problems, why her last thing
ended. It reads as intensity she did not sign up for, and it is hard to
walk back.

**Lines.** A [pick up line](/blog/pick-up-line) can open. It cannot
sustain. Ten messages of material is a conversation, not a routine.

## How to tell a topic is landing

You do not have to guess. The thread tells you, and the signal is
length, not enthusiasm.

If she answers with more words than you sent, the topic is working —
stay on it. People write long when they are interested, and short when
they are being polite. If her replies shrink across three messages in a
row, the topic is done. Change it without commenting on it. Never
announce the change ("anyway, different subject") — just react to
something else she said earlier and keep moving.

Trailing punctuation and one-word replies are their own signal, and
usually not the one guys read them as. There is a full breakdown of
[what "..." means in a text from a girl](/blog/meaning-in-text-from-a-girl),
but the short version: it is rarely anger and usually low investment.
Treat it as a prompt to change direction, not to ask what is wrong.

The other thing worth watching is who is asking. If every question in
the last twenty messages came from you, the conversation is not mutual
yet, no matter how friendly it reads. Try going quiet for a beat and
letting her carry one.

## Know when to stop talking and ask

Good conversation has a shelf life. Keep it going too long and the
thread becomes the relationship — pleasant, endless, going nowhere, and
eventually she matches with someone who suggested a drink on day two.

A reasonable marker is twenty to thirty messages, or the first time she
laughs at something you said and asks you something back unprompted.
That is the moment to move it, and
[how to ask a girl out on Tinder](/blog/ask-girl-out-tinder) covers the
phrasing that makes it easy to say yes to. If where this is heading is
more casual on both sides, the same timing logic applies to
[reading what she actually wants](/blog/how-to-hookup-with-a-girl).

Texting is not the goal. It is the part you do until there is a reason
to meet.

## When the topic is not the problem

One caveat, because it saves a lot of wasted effort.

If conversations die with every match, at the same point, regardless of
what you talk about, the problem is upstream of your messages. Usually
it is match quality — the profile is pulling in people who swiped right
casually and are not invested enough to sustain anything. No topic
fixes a thread that was never warm.

Guys spend weeks rewriting their messaging when the thing that moved
the numbers was always the profile. If your replies die early and often,
start there and come back to this. The tell is consistency: a messaging
problem varies by match, and a profile problem does not.

## The short version

What to talk about with a girl comes down to picking subjects that
open rather than close. React to what she volunteered, ask for opinions
that cost her nothing, get specific about her week, and tease her about
patterns she did not notice she had.

Watch reply length to know if it is working. Drop topics quietly when
they are not. And once she is writing more than you are, stop finding
new things to talk about and ask her out.
