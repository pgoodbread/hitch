Tinder Boost is the purple button that promises to put you at the front
of the queue for thirty minutes. You get one free with Gold, you can
buy them individually, and the app is very good at offering you one
right after a dry spell.

The short version: a Boost works, in the narrow sense that it does what
it says. It shows your profile to more people, fast. Whether that turns
into matches is a completely different question, and the answer depends
on something the Boost doesn't touch.

## What a Tinder Boost actually does

For thirty minutes, Tinder pushes your profile toward the top of the
stack for people swiping near you. You become one of the more visible
profiles in your area for the duration.

That's it. It is a visibility product, not a matching product.

Two things follow from that, and they matter more than any tactic in
this article:

**A Boost does not change who sees you**, in the sense of type or
compatibility. It changes how many people in your existing radius and
filter range get your card in front of them during that window. Same
audience, more of it, sooner.

**A Boost does not change your profile.** The people who see you during
those thirty minutes are looking at exactly the same photos and bio
that everyone else saw yesterday. If they swiped left yesterday, a
Boosted version of the same profile gets the same answer.

While it's running you'll see a little counter telling you how many
times your profile is being viewed. That number is reach, not interest,
and confusing the two is how people end up disappointed.

## The math that decides whether it's worth it

This is the whole thing, and it takes one paragraph.

A Boost multiplies impressions. Your profile converts those
impressions into right-swipes at some rate. Matches are impressions
times rate. If 100 people see you and three swipe right, ten times the
visibility gets you thirty. If the rate is closer to zero, ten times
the visibility gets you ten times more nothing — you've just bought a
larger audience for the same rejection.

So the question isn't whether Boost works. It's whether you're at the
stage where reach is your bottleneck. If you get matches and just want
more of them, a Boost is a reasonable purchase. If matches aren't
happening at all, you have a conversion problem, and no amount of
visibility fixes conversion.

## What a Boost costs

Tinder prices this the way it prices everything: by age bracket, by
country, by how many you buy at once, and with constant promotions.

Gold and Platinum include one Boost a month. Beyond that you buy them
as credits, and the per-Boost price drops sharply when you buy a pack
of five or ten instead of one. Realistically a single Boost lands in
the few-dollars range in the US, less per unit in a pack, with real
variation elsewhere.

Check what your account is being offered. That's the only price that's
accurate, and it's usually different from whatever number an article
quotes you. If you already pay for
[Gold, whether that tier is worth it has its own honest answer](/blog/is-tinder-gold-worth-it)
— and the monthly Boost is one of the smaller reasons to buy it.

## Boost vs Super Boost

Tinder sells a bigger version of the same idea. Super Boost runs longer
and promises far more visibility than a standard Boost, and it costs
correspondingly more. It's usually offered only at certain times, in
certain markets, rather than being available on demand.

The logic doesn't change with the size. Super Boost is a larger
multiplier on the same conversion rate, which means it's a better deal
than a regular Boost if your profile already works and a more expensive
mistake if it doesn't. Buy the small one first and see what it returns
before you scale the spend.

## The best time to run a Tinder boost

If you're going to spend one, spend it when the most people are
swiping. More people online means your thirty minutes of priority gets
shown to more of them.

**Sunday evening is the reliable one.** Roughly 7pm to 10pm local.
Sunday night is consistently one of the heaviest swiping windows of the
week — people are home, the weekend is over, and the week ahead hasn't
started.

**Weekday evenings, 8pm to 10pm.** The commute-home-and-scroll window.
Not as strong as Sunday, but dependable.

**Not at 3am, and not at 11am on a Tuesday.** Obvious, but the free
monthly Boost tends to get spent whenever someone happens to open the
app and notice it.

**Be there to reply.** This is the part people skip. A Boost gets you
matches in a burst, and a match is most likely to reply while she's
still in the app. Running a Boost and then going to bed wastes half of
what you bought. Have thirty minutes free afterwards.

One more: if you're travelling or just moved, a Boost in a new city is
worth more than it is at home, because nobody there has seen your
profile yet.

## What Boost can't fix

**A weak first photo.** Boost buys impressions; the first photo decides
what those impressions convert at. It's the only image most people ever
see, and
[that one decision carries the profile](/blog/why-your-first-tinder-photo-decides-everything).
Buying reach before fixing it is paying to show a bad photo to more
people.

**A thin profile.** If your photos are a blurry group shot and your bio
is empty, the problem is the asset, not the distribution. A
[decent set of photos](/blog/profile-pictures) is a weekend and a
friend with a phone, and working from a
[profile template](/blog/tinder-profile-template) — a fixed photo order
with a job for each slot, and a bio in three lines — gets you a
competent profile in an afternoon. Both are free, and both raise the
rate that every future Boost multiplies.

**Conversations that die.** Boost gets you the match. What happens next
is entirely on you, and a lot of guys buy reach when their actual leak
is the first message.
[An opener that goes somewhere](/blog/tinder-openers) is a different
skill from getting matched, and it's the one that decides whether any
of this turns into a date.

**A too-narrow filter.** If your age range and distance settings leave
you a small pool, priority within that small pool doesn't help much.
Widen the settings before you spend.

## Does Boost hurt your profile afterwards?

There's a persistent theory that Tinder suppresses your profile after a
Boost so you'll buy another one. Tinder says no, and there's no
evidence for it beyond the fact that any burst of activity feels like a
drop when it ends.

What's actually happening is simpler. A Boost front-loads thirty
minutes' worth of the people near you. Afterwards, some meaningful
share of your local pool has already seen and decided on your profile.
Fewer new people are left to show it to, so the following days feel
quiet. That's not a penalty, it's arithmetic — and it's also why
Boosting repeatedly in the same small town has sharply diminishing
returns.

If you want the mechanics of the queue itself, the swipes are worth
understanding first —
[what a left swipe actually signals](/blog/swipe-left-meaning) explains
more about your visibility than any paid feature does.

## The short version

A Tinder Boost does exactly what it claims: thirty minutes near the top
of the stack. It's a multiplier on a match rate you already have, which
makes it a decent buy if your profile converts and a waste if it
doesn't.

Run it Sunday evening, be around to reply for the half hour after, and
don't expect it to change anything structural. Fix the first photo and
the bio first — they cost nothing, they work permanently, and they're
what decides whether the reach you bought was worth buying.
