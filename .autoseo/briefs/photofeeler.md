## Angle

People searching "photofeeler" are trying to answer one question: _which of my
photos should I actually use?_ Photofeeler is a crowd-rating tool — strangers
score your photo on Smart / Trustworthy / Attractive. That is genuinely useful,
but it was built for LinkedIn headshots and general social profiles, not for
the specific job of a Tinder lineup: which photo goes first, what the other
five do, and whether the set as a whole reads as one coherent person.

So the article should be an honest, useful review — what Photofeeler is, how it
works, what the scores mean, what it costs, where it's strong, and where it
falls short for dating specifically — and then cover the alternatives (Hitch
included, plainly and without overselling). Do not trash Photofeeler; it's a
real tool and readers will notice bias immediately. Win by being the most
complete answer, not the most promotional one.

Cover at minimum:

- What Photofeeler is and how the crowd-voting model works
- The three score dimensions and how to read percentiles (not raw scores)
- Pricing / free-karma model, and how long results take
- Is it accurate? — sample-size and voter-demographic caveats
- Where it's weak for Tinder: rates one photo at a time, no lineup ordering,
  no bio/prompt context, voters aren't your target demographic
- Alternatives, including Hitch, plus the free option (asking friends — and why
  that's biased)
- A short "how to actually use photo feedback" section: test the first photo
  hardest, keep one clear face shot, vary context, kill the group shot
