## Why this angle

The searcher is an active Tinder user who has sent a message and heard nothing
back. The literal question ("do read receipts exist?") is a 40-word answer, so
the article earns its length by answering the question the searcher actually
has: _did she see it, and if she did, why didn't she reply?_ Answer the factual
question fast and completely up top — that is what wins the snippet — then move
to the diagnosis. Do not bury the direct answer.

Cover, roughly in this order:

- The direct answer: whether Tinder has read receipts, and that they are a paid
  à-la-carte add-on rather than a standard feature (Tinder has shipped these as
  purchasable Read Receipts; Gold/Platinum tiers have bundled credits at times).
  State pricing and packaging as "varies by region and changes often" rather
  than quoting a hard number you cannot verify — do not invent prices, credit
  counts, or tier bundles.
- What a read receipt does and does not tell you: it confirms delivery and
  opening, not interest.
- Why buying them is usually a bad trade: it spends money to confirm a bad
  outcome instead of fixing the cause.
- How to read the signals you already get free: match-to-reply ratio, whether
  she unmatched, whether she is still showing in the match list, typing
  indicators, response latency patterns.
- The real diagnosis section: what "seen and ignored" actually means. The
  opener, the profile behind the opener, the photo that set the expectation.
- What to do next: rewrite the opener, fix the first photo, and when to move on.
